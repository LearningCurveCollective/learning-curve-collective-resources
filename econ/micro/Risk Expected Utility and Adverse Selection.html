The Formulas

Expected Value (average dollars)

$$EV_B = P_h \cdot RP_B^h + P_l \cdot RP_B^l$$

A weighted average, same math as grade weights. A risk neutral buyer pays up to $EV_B$ for a random unit.

Expected Utility (average happiness)

$$EU = P_1\,U(V_1) + P_2\,U(V_2)$$

Utility first, then average. Each outcome goes through $U$ before you weight it.

Certainty Equivalent

$$U(CE) = EU$$

Average happiness equals what value? Undo the utility to get back to dollars. With a utility function, the buyer's max price is $CE$, not $EV$.

Risk Premium

$$RP = EV - CE$$

The dollars a risk averse person gives up to kill the uncertainty. Always positive when $U$ is concave.

Does a trade happen? (perfect info)

$$RP_B \ge RP_S$$

Check type by type. If the buyer's max beats the seller's min, there is a price both sides take.

Do both types sell? (asymmetric info)

$$WTP \ge RP_S^h$$

$WTP = EV_B$ if risk neutral, $WTP = CE$ if there is a utility function. If it fails, high quality sellers walk.

Minimum fraction of high quality

$$WTP(P) = RP_S^h \;\Rightarrow\; \text{solve for } P$$

Risk neutral: $P \cdot RP_B^h + (1-P)RP_B^l = RP_S^h$. Risk averse: same equation with $U(\cdot)$ wrapped around every number.

Risk attitude test

$$U(EV) \;\text{vs.}\; EU$$

$U(EV) > EU$: risk averse. Equal: risk neutral. $U(EV) < EU$: risk loving.

The one phrase that answers most of this unit: average happiness equals what value? Find the expected utility, then ask what sure amount of money produces that same happiness. That sure amount is the certainty equivalent, and it is the price a risk averse person will actually pay.

Code Language: You Read This, You Set It Up Like That

When the problem says...Your setup is...
"Both buyers and sellers know quality with certainty" / "perfect information" Two separate markets, no probabilities. Check $RP_B \ge RP_S$ for each type on its own.
"Buyers cannot tell, but sellers know" Asymmetric information. Buyers price a random unit: compute $WTP$ and compare it to $RP_S^h$. If $WTP < RP_S^h$, high quality exits (adverse selection).
"Buyers are risk neutral... maximum they will spend is the expected value" $WTP = EV_B = P_h \cdot RP_B^h + P_l \cdot RP_B^l$
"Suppose they have the utility function $U(f) = \ldots$" Risk averse now. Compute $EU$, then solve $U(WTP) = EU$. Average happiness equals what value?
"Calculate the minimum fraction of the market..." Code for: solve for $P$. Pretend you were never told what $P$ is. Set $WTP(P) = RP_S^h$ and solve.
"What minimum probability must he believe...?" (someone takes a bet) $EU(\text{take the bet}) = U(\text{wealth with no bet})$. Solve for $P$.
"Calculate the certainty equivalent" $U(CE) = EU$, then undo the utility. Square root utility: square both sides. Cube root: cube both sides.
"Would she accept \$X for certain instead of the gamble?" Accept if $X \ge CE$. Same test in happiness units: compare $U(X)$ to $EU$.
"Suppose instead utility were $V(w) = a + b\,U(w)$ with $b > 0$" Nothing changes. Shifting or scaling the happiness units keeps every ranking the same, so every choice stays the same.

4

The Three Scenarios

1. Perfect information
Everyone sees quality. Each type trades in its own market, so probability never enters. Check $RP_B \ge RP_S$ type by type and you are done.
2. Risk with symmetric information
Nobody can tell the types apart, buyers or sellers. Both sides price the same average unit, so the market still clears as long as the buyers' average beats the sellers' average.
3. Risk with asymmetric information
Sellers know what they have, buyers do not. Buyers offer an average price, high quality sellers refuse it, and the market unravels into lemons. This is adverse selection.
5

The Rounding Rule

When you subtract numbers that sit close together, keep 4 to 6 decimals until the last step. Logs of similar wealth levels are the classic trap:

Rounded to 2 decimals: $\;13.82 - 13.71 = 0.11$ and $13.91 - 13.71 = 0.20$, so $P = 0.11/0.20 = 0.55$.

Kept at 6 decimals: $\;13.815511 - 13.710150 = 0.105361$ and $13.910821 - 13.710150 = 0.200671$, so $P = 0.5250$.

Subtraction cancels most of the digits, so a tiny rounding error becomes a big share of what is left. Round at the end, not in the middle.

"Average Happiness Equals What Value?" Worked Once

The refrigerator numbers you know: high quality is worth \$5,000 to buyers, low quality \$2,500, each type is half the market, and $U(f) = \sqrt{f}$. What will a buyer pay for a random fridge?

First, the average happiness. Utility of each outcome, then the weighted average:

$$EU_B = 0.5\sqrt{5000} + 0.5\sqrt{2500} = 0.5(70.7107) + 0.5(50) = 60.3553$$

Then, what value gives that happiness? Set the utility of the unknown price equal to it and undo the square root:

$$\sqrt{WTP} = 60.3553 \;\Rightarrow\; WTP = (60.3553)^2 = \$3{,}642.77$$

That is the whole move. Expected utility gets you into happiness units; the certainty equivalent gets you back out to dollars. Last step is the market question: $3{,}642.77 < RP_S^h = 4{,}500$, so high quality sellers walk away and only lemons trade.

Why expected value is just an average. Tests worth 50% of your grade, homework 30%, participation 20%: your course grade is a weighted average. A gamble with a 50% outcome, a 30% outcome, and a 20% outcome is the identical calculation. The probabilities are the weights. You will never land exactly on the average in one play, but it is the fair price of the game if you could play it over and over.

What Risk Aversion Does to the Market

Risk averse buyers feel losses more than gains, so a random unit is worth less to them than its average. Same fridge mix, two different buyers:

Risk neutralRisk averse ($U = \sqrt{f}$)
Buyer's max price for a random unit$EV_B = \$3{,}750$$CE = \$3{,}642.77$
Minimum fraction high quality$P \ge 0.80$$P \ge 0.8248$

The gap between the two prices (\$107.23) is the risk premium. Because risk averse buyers offer less for the same mix, the market needs even fewer lemons to hold together. Every part (d) and (e) on this topic is testing whether you see that direction.

The Used E-Bike Market

1

A resale platform sells used e-bikes. There are two types: high quality ($h$) and low quality ($l$). The lowest price sellers will accept and the highest price buyers will pay are shown below. All market participants know that 50 percent of e-bikes are high quality and 50 percent are low quality.

Maximum price buyers are willing to payMinimum price sellers are willing to accept
High quality e-bike ($h$)\$3,600\$3,000
Low quality e-bike ($l$)\$1,600\$1,200
Type$P$$RP_B$$RP_S$
$h$$0.5$\$3,600\$3,000
$l$$0.5$\$1,600\$1,200

Worth computing now since you know it is coming: $EV_B = 0.5(3600) + 0.5(1600) = \$2{,}600$.

(a) Suppose buyers and sellers are equally well informed and both know with certainty whether a given e-bike is high or low quality. Will both types be sold in this perfect information equilibrium? Explain briefly.

Perfect information: no probabilities, two separate markets. Check $RP_B \ge RP_S$ for each type on its own.

Market $h$: $\;RP_B^h = 3600 > RP_S^h = 3000 \;\Rightarrow\; 3000 \le \text{Price}_h \le 3600$  ✓ trade

Market $l$: $\;RP_B^l = 1600 > RP_S^l = 1200 \;\Rightarrow\; 1200 \le \text{Price}_l \le 1600$  ✓ trade

Yes, both types sell. Buyers can tell the types apart, so each type gets its own price, and in each market the buyer's max beats the seller's min. The 50/50 split never comes into it.

(b) Now suppose buyers cannot tell if a given e-bike is high or low quality, but sellers know. Buyers are risk neutral, and the maximum a buyer will spend is the expected value of a random e-bike. Will both types be sold? What happens to the price over time?

Asymmetric information, risk neutral: $WTP = EV_B$. Compare it to $RP_S^h$ first, then think about what buyers learn once the high quality sellers react.

$$EV_B = 0.5(3600) + 0.5(1600) = 1800 + 800 = \$2{,}600$$

Check each seller against the \$2,600 offer: $\;2600 < RP_S^h = 3000$, so high quality sellers refuse to sell. $\;2600 > RP_S^l = 1200$, so low quality sellers happily take it.

No, only low quality e-bikes sell. Over time buyers notice every bike they get is a lemon, so they update to $P_l = 1$ and cut their offer to $RP_B^l = 1600$. The price settles between \$1,200 and \$1,600, and the \$600 of surplus per high quality bike is lost. Adverse selection.

(c) Calculate the minimum fraction of the e-bike market that must be high quality to support an equilibrium where both types are sold.

"Minimum fraction" is code for solving for $P$. Pretend nobody told you $P = 0.5$. Set $EV_B(P) = RP_S^h$ and solve. (Why $RP_S^h$ and not $RP_B^h$? The binding constraint is getting high quality sellers to stay in.)

$$3600P + 1600(1 - P) \ge 3000$$ $$1600 + 2000P \ge 3000$$ $$2000P \ge 1400 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.70$$

At least 70% of the market must be high quality. Check: $0.7(3600) + 0.3(1600) = 2520 + 480 = 3000$, exactly the high quality seller's floor. In part (b), $P = 0.5$ fell short of this, which is why the market unraveled.

(d) Suppose instead that buyers and sellers have the utility function $U(f) = f^{1/2}$, where $f$ is the value of the e-bike. What is the maximum amount a buyer will pay for an e-bike chosen at random? Will both types be sold?

Average happiness equals what value? Find $EU$ first (utility of each outcome, then the weighted average), then undo the square root to get back to dollars.

Step 1: average happiness. $\sqrt{3600} = 60$ and $\sqrt{1600} = 40$, so:

$$EU_B = 0.5(60) + 0.5(40) = 50$$

Step 2: what value gives that happiness?

$$\sqrt{WTP} = 50 \;\Rightarrow\; WTP = 50^2 = \$2{,}500$$

Step 3: check the sellers. $2500 < RP_S^h = 3000$, so high quality sellers still walk. $2500 > RP_S^l = 1200$, so lemons trade.

The max price is \$2,500, and only low quality sells. Note the direction: risk neutral buyers offered \$2,600, risk averse buyers offer \$2,500. The \$100 gap is the risk premium, and it makes the adverse selection worse.

(e) Given this utility function, what is the minimum fraction of the market that must be high quality to support an equilibrium where both types are sold?

Same equation as (c), but wrap a square root around every dollar figure and work in happiness units: $EU(P) = \sqrt{RP_S^h}$. Sellers share the utility function here, but since $\sqrt{\cdot}$ keeps order, the high quality seller's floor is still \$3,000.

$$60P + 40(1 - P) \ge \sqrt{3000} = 54.7723$$ $$40 + 20P \ge 54.7723$$ $$20P \ge 14.7723 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.7386$$

Check: at $P = 0.7386$, $\;EU = 0.7386(60) + 0.2614(40) = 54.7723$, and $54.7723^2 = 3{,}000$. ✓

At least about 73.86% must be high quality, up from 70% in the risk neutral case. Risk aversion lowers what buyers offer for the same mix, so the market tolerates fewer lemons. Keep $\sqrt{3000}$ at 4+ decimals until the end.

The Even-Money Bet

2

Priya is seen placing an even-money bet of \$40,000 that LA wins the cup. Her initial wealth is \$200,000, and her utility over wealth is $U(W) = \ln(W)$.

(Note: an even-money bet means she either wins \$40,000 or loses \$40,000.)

OutcomeWealth$\ln(W)$, kept to 6 decimals
Win (prob $P$)\$240,000$12.388394$
Lose (prob $1-P$)\$160,000$11.982929$
No bet\$200,000$12.206073$

(a) What must she believe to be the minimum probability that LA wins?

She took the bet, so the bet must feel at least as good as not betting: $EU(\text{bet}) \ge U(200{,}000)$. Plug numbers into each $\ln$, no log rules, and keep 6 decimals since the logs sit close together.

$$P\ln(240{,}000) + (1-P)\ln(160{,}000) \ge \ln(200{,}000)$$ $$P(12.388394) + (1-P)(11.982929) \ge 12.206073$$

Distribute the $(1-P)$ and collect:

$$12.388394P + 11.982929 - 11.982929P \ge 12.206073$$ $$0.405465P \ge 0.223144$$ $$P \ge \frac{0.223144}{0.405465} \approx 0.5503$$

She must believe LA wins with probability of at least about 55.03%. Rounding check: if you had rounded the logs to 2 decimals, the numerator becomes $0.11$ and the denominator $0.20$, and you would report 55% by luck of cancellation on a problem this size, but on tighter numbers it flips the answer. Keep the decimals.

(b) What minimum probability would a risk neutral Priya need? Why is your answer to (a) higher?

Risk neutral means she only cares about expected dollars: $EV(\text{bet}) \ge 0$.

$$P(40{,}000) - (1-P)(40{,}000) \ge 0 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.50$$

Risk neutral: anything above 50%. The answer in (a) is higher because $\ln(W)$ is concave, so losing \$40,000 costs her more happiness than winning \$40,000 adds. She needs about a 5 point edge in her beliefs before the gamble is worth taking.

Dev's Gamble

3

Dev faces the following gamble over wealth: 50% chance of \$100, 50% chance of \$400. His utility function is $U(w) = w^{1/2}$.

Outcome$P$$w$$U(w) = \sqrt{w}$
Low$0.5$\$100$10$
High$0.5$\$400$20$

Both wealth levels are perfect squares, so the utilities are exact. That is your hint that the arithmetic should stay clean.

(a) Calculate Dev's expected wealth.

$$EV = 0.5(100) + 0.5(400) = 50 + 200 = \$250$$

(b) Calculate his expected utility from the gamble.

Utility of each outcome first, then the weighted average. Do not average the wealth first.

$$EU = 0.5\sqrt{100} + 0.5\sqrt{400} = 0.5(10) + 0.5(20) = 15$$

(c) Calculate the utility Dev would receive if he were given the expected value of the gamble for certain. Is he risk averse, risk neutral, or risk loving? Explain.

He is handed \$250 for sure, so just run 250 through the utility function. Then compare $U(EV)$ to $EU$ from part (b). This is the opposite order of operations from (b): average first, then utility.

$$U(EV) = \sqrt{250} = 15.8114 \;>\; EU = 15$$

Risk averse. A sure \$250 makes him happier than a gamble averaging \$250. Square root utility is concave: the drop from \$250 to \$100 hurts more than the jump from \$250 to \$400 helps.

(d) Calculate the certainty equivalent of the gamble.

Average happiness equals what value? Set $U(CE)$ equal to the expected utility from (b) and undo the square root.

$$\sqrt{CE} = 15 \;\Rightarrow\; CE = 15^2 = \$225$$

A sure \$225 feels exactly as good as the gamble. The risk premium is $EV - CE = 250 - 225 = \$25$, the amount of expected wealth he would give up to escape the uncertainty.

(e) Suppose Dev is offered \$240 for certain instead of the gamble. Would he accept it? Explain.

Compare the sure offer to the certainty equivalent. Or do it in happiness units: compare $U(240)$ to $EU$.

$$240 > CE = 225 \quad\text{(or } \sqrt{240} = 15.4919 > EU = 15\text{)}$$

Yes, he accepts. Notice \$240 is below his expected wealth of \$250, and he takes it anyway. That is risk aversion in action: any sure amount above \$225 beats the gamble for him.

(f) Suppose instead that Dev's utility function were $V(w) = 3 + 5w^{1/2}$. Would his choices change? Explain briefly.

Look at the shape of $V$: it is the old utility multiplied by a positive number, plus a constant. What does that do to rankings? If unsure, recompute the $CE$ under $V$ and see.

No, nothing changes. $V$ rescales the happiness units (times 5, plus 3), like switching a thermometer from Celsius to Fahrenheit. Whatever ranked higher before still ranks higher.

Check it with the certainty equivalent. $V(100) = 3 + 5(10) = 53$ and $V(400) = 3 + 5(20) = 103$, so:

$$E[V] = 0.5(53) + 0.5(103) = 78$$ $$3 + 5\sqrt{CE} = 78 \;\Rightarrow\; \sqrt{CE} = 15 \;\Rightarrow\; CE = \$225$$

Same \$225 as before, so every accept-or-reject decision comes out identical. The happiness numbers changed; the choices did not.

(d) Calculate the certainty equivalent and the risk premium of the gamble.

Average happiness equals what value? Cube root utility means you cube both sides to undo it.

$$CE^{1/3} = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; CE = 4^3 = \$64$$ $$RP = EV - CE = 76 - 64 = \$12$$

A sure \$64 feels the same as the gamble, and she would give up \$12 of expected wealth to escape the risk.

(e) Suppose Noor is offered \$70 for certain instead of the gamble. Does she accept? Explain.

Compare the sure offer to the certainty equivalent, or $U(70)$ to $EU$.

$$70 > CE = 64 \quad\text{(or } 70^{1/3} = 4.1213 > EU = 4\text{)}$$

Yes, she accepts, even though \$70 is below her expected wealth of \$76. Any sure amount above \$64 beats the gamble for her.

(f) Suppose instead that her utility were $V(w) = 10 + 3w^{1/3}$. Would her choices change? Explain briefly.

$V$ is the old utility times a positive number, plus a constant. What does that do to rankings? If unsure, recompute the $CE$ under $V$.

No, nothing changes. It is a relabeling of the happiness scale. Check with the certainty equivalent: $V(27) = 10 + 3(3) = 19$ and $V(125) = 10 + 3(5) = 25$, so:

$$E[V] = 0.5(19) + 0.5(25) = 22$$ $$10 + 3\,CE^{1/3} = 22 \;\Rightarrow\; CE^{1/3} = 4 \;\Rightarrow\; CE = \$64$$

Same \$64, so every accept-or-reject decision is identical.

The Road Bike Market (Watch the Probabilities)

1

A consignment shop sells used road bikes. There are two types: high quality ($h$) and low quality ($l$). All market participants know that 40 percent of the bikes are high quality and 60 percent are low quality.

Maximum price buyers are willing to payMinimum price sellers are willing to accept
High quality bike ($h$)\$4,900\$3,700
Low quality bike ($l$)\$2,500\$1,900
Type$P$$RP_B$$RP_S$
$h$$0.4$\$4,900\$3,700
$l$$0.6$\$2,500\$1,900

The split is not 50/50 this time. Half the mistakes on problems like this come from writing $0.5$ on autopilot.

(a) Under perfect information, will both types be sold?

Market $h$: $\;4900 > 3700$  ✓ trade

Market $l$: $\;2500 > 1900$  ✓ trade

Yes, both sell. With perfect information the 40/60 split is irrelevant.

(b) Buyers cannot tell the types apart, sellers can, and buyers are risk neutral. Will both types be sold?

$WTP = EV_B$, with the weights the problem actually gave you: 0.4 and 0.6.

$$EV_B = 0.4(4900) + 0.6(2500) = 1960 + 1500 = \$3{,}460$$

$3460 < RP_S^h = 3700$, so only low quality bikes sell, and the price eventually settles between \$1,900 and \$2,500.

(c) Calculate the minimum fraction of the market that must be high quality for both types to sell. How does it compare to the actual 40%?

Solve for $P$: set $EV_B(P) = 3700$. The 40% given in the problem plays no role in this equation; it only matters for the comparison afterward.

$$4900P + 2500(1 - P) \ge 3700$$ $$2500 + 2400P \ge 3700$$ $$2400P \ge 1200 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.50$$

The market needs at least 50% high quality, and it only has 40%. That gap is exactly why (b) unraveled.

(d) Now suppose buyers and sellers have utility $U(f) = f^{1/2}$. What is the maximum a buyer pays for a random bike? Will both types sell?

Average happiness equals what value, with the 0.4/0.6 weights.

$\sqrt{4900} = 70$ and $\sqrt{2500} = 50$, so:

$$EU_B = 0.4(70) + 0.6(50) = 28 + 30 = 58$$ $$\sqrt{WTP} = 58 \;\Rightarrow\; WTP = 58^2 = \$3{,}364$$

The max price is \$3,364, still below \$3,700, so only low quality sells. The risk premium is $3460 - 3364 = \$96$.

(e) Given this utility function, what is the minimum fraction of high quality bikes needed for both types to sell?

Happiness units: $EU(P) = \sqrt{3700}$. Keep the square root at 4+ decimals.

$$70P + 50(1 - P) \ge \sqrt{3700} = 60.8276$$ $$50 + 20P \ge 60.8276$$ $$20P \ge 10.8276 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.5414$$

At least about 54.14%, up from 50%. Same direction as always: risk aversion shrinks the number of lemons a market can carry.

The Bet with Square Root Utility

2

Sasha has wealth of \$42,500 and utility $U(W) = W^{1/2}$. She is offered an even-money bet of \$20,000 on the outcome of a match.

OutcomeWealth$\sqrt{W}$
Win (prob $P$)\$62,500$250$
Lose (prob $1-P$)\$22,500$150$
No bet\$42,500$206.1553$

Same structure as the $\ln$ bets, different utility function. The method does not care which $U$ you were handed.

(a) What minimum probability of winning does she need to take the bet?

$EU(\text{bet}) \ge U(42{,}500)$. Same equation as the $\ln$ problems, just with square roots.

$$P(250) + (1-P)(150) \ge 206.1553$$ $$150 + 100P \ge 206.1553$$ $$100P \ge 56.1553 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.5616$$

She needs at least about a 56.16% chance of winning. Big bet relative to her wealth, so the required edge is large.

(b) Suppose the match is actually a fair coin flip ($P = 0.5$) and she takes the bet anyway. Find the certainty equivalent of her post-bet wealth and her risk premium. What do they say about the deal she made?

Her post-bet wealth is a 50/50 gamble over \$62,500 and \$22,500. Find $EU$, then the value that gives that happiness, then compare to her \$42,500.

$$EU = 0.5(250) + 0.5(150) = 200$$ $$\sqrt{CE} = 200 \;\Rightarrow\; CE = \$40{,}000$$ $$RP = EV - CE = 42{,}500 - 40{,}000 = \$2{,}500$$

Her expected wealth is unchanged at \$42,500 (fair bet), but in happiness terms the gamble is only worth a sure \$40,000 to her. Taking a fair 50/50 bet cost her the equivalent of \$2,500. That is why a risk averse person never takes a fair bet, and why (a) demanded an edge.

The Vintage Guitar Market (Big Spread)

3

A vintage shop sells used guitars: genuine vintage instruments ($h$) and ordinary ones dressed up to look vintage ($l$). Half of each. Buyers cannot tell them apart; sellers can.

Maximum price buyers are willing to payMinimum price sellers are willing to accept
Genuine vintage ($h$)\$8,000\$5,000
Dressed up ($l$)\$1,000\$800
Type$P$$RP_B$$RP_S$
$h$$0.5$\$8,000\$5,000
$l$$0.5$\$1,000\$800

Notice how far apart the two types sit. Keep an eye on what that spread does to the answers.

(a) Buyers are risk neutral. Will both types be sold?

$$EV_B = 0.5(8000) + 0.5(1000) = \$4{,}500 < RP_S^h = \$5{,}000$$

No, only the dressed up guitars sell. Note the genuine article is worth \$3,000 more to buyers than sellers need, and that surplus still dies.

(b) Calculate the minimum fraction of genuine guitars needed for both types to sell, with risk neutral buyers.

Solve for $P$: $EV_B(P) = 5000$.

$$8000P + 1000(1 - P) \ge 5000$$ $$1000 + 7000P \ge 5000$$ $$7000P \ge 4000 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.5714$$

At least about 57.14% genuine.

(c) Now buyers and sellers have utility $U(f) = f^{1/3}$. What is the maximum a buyer pays for a random guitar? Will both types sell?

Cube root this time: $8000^{1/3} = 20$ and $1000^{1/3} = 10$. Average happiness equals what value, then cube both sides.

$$EU_B = 0.5(20) + 0.5(10) = 15$$ $$WTP^{1/3} = 15 \;\Rightarrow\; WTP = 15^3 = \$3{,}375$$

The max price is \$3,375, and only the dressed up guitars sell. Compare to the risk neutral \$4,500: the risk premium here is \$1,125, enormous because the two outcomes sit so far apart. Big spread, big risk, big discount.

(d) Given this utility function, what is the minimum fraction of genuine guitars needed for both types to sell? Compare it to (b).

Happiness units: $EU(P) = 5000^{1/3}$. Keep the cube root at 4+ decimals.

$$20P + 10(1 - P) \ge 5000^{1/3} = 17.0998$$ $$10 + 10P \ge 17.0998$$ $$10P \ge 7.0998 \;\Rightarrow\; P \ge 0.7100$$

At least about 71%, versus 57.14% when risk neutral. That is a 14 point jump, much larger than in the e-bike or laptop markets, because the wider the gap between the good and bad outcome, the more a risk averse buyer discounts the gamble. Spread drives the size of the risk premium, and the risk premium drives how fragile the market is.
