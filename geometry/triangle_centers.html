Triangle Centers

Centroid · Incenter · Circumcenter · Orthocenter
G
Centroid
built from: Medians

The point where the three medians meet (a median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side). It's the triangle's balance point — cut the triangle from cardboard and it balances on G. It always sits inside the triangle, and it divides each median in a 2:1 ratio (vertex side is the long part).

I
Incenter
built from: Angle bisectors

The point where the three angle bisectors meet (each bisector splits a vertex angle in half). It's the center of the incircle — the biggest circle that fits inside the triangle, touching all three sides. Equidistant from the three sides. Always inside the triangle.

O
Circumcenter
built from: Perpendicular bisectors

The point where the three perpendicular bisectors of the sides meet (each passes through a side's midpoint at 90°). It's the center of the circumcircle — the circle through all three vertices. Equidistant from the three vertices. Lands inside an acute triangle, on the hypotenuse's midpoint for a right triangle, and outside an obtuse one.

H
Orthocenter
built from: Altitudes

The point where the three altitudes meet (an altitude drops from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side). Inside an acute triangle, exactly at the right-angle vertex for a right triangle, and outside an obtuse one. Fun fact: G always sits on the line between O and H — the Euler line.

Try it — drag the vertices

Toggle the centers on and off, then drag A, B, or C and watch how each center moves.
Watch: in a right triangle, O lands on the hypotenuse's midpoint and H lands on the right-angle corner. In an obtuse one, both escape outside. In an equilateral triangle, all four centers stack on one point.
