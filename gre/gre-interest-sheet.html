Interest rules

Every interest question is a percent-change question wearing a banking costume. In the rules below, $P$ is the starting amount (the principal), $r$ is the annual rate written as a decimal, $t$ is time in years, and $V$ is the value at the end. Read slowly; each rule comes with worked examples.

Rule 1Percent change as a multiplier:   $r\%$ up: $\times\left(1 + \tfrac{r}{100}\right)$    $r\%$ down: $\times\left(1 - \tfrac{r}{100}\right)$
Example Aa 5% increase: $\times 1.05$
Example Ba 20% decrease: $\times 0.80$
Example Csuccessive changes multiply: up 10% then down 10% is $\times 1.10 \times 0.90 = \times 0.99$
Rule 2Simple interest:   $I = Prt \qquad V = P + I \;\Longleftrightarrow\; V = P(1 + rt)$
Example A\$2,000 at 4% for 3 years: $I = 2000 \times 0.04 \times 3 = \$240$, so $V = \$2,240$
Example Btime converts to years: \$9,000 at 4% for 6 months gives $I = 9000 \times 0.04 \times \tfrac{1}{2} = \$180$
Rule 3Compound interest, annual:   $V = P(1 + r)^{\,t}$
Example A\$1,000 at 5% for 2 years: $1000(1.05)^2 = \$1,102.50$
Simple interest would give \$1,100 — the extra \$2.50 is interest earned on year one's interest. That gap is the entire difference between the two formulas.
Rule 4Compounded n times per year:   $V = P\left(1 + \tfrac{r}{n}\right)^{\,nt}$
Example A8% compounded semiannually for 1 year: the per-period rate is 4% and there are 2 periods, so $V = P(1.04)^2$
Example B12% compounded monthly for 2 years: $V = P(1.01)^{24}$
Rule 5Solving backwards:   $P = \dfrac{V}{(1+r)^{\,t}}$, and after one year $r = \dfrac{V}{P} - 1$
Example Aa value of \$1,060 after 1 year at 6% simple came from $P = \dfrac{1060}{1.06} = \$1,000$
Example B\$2,000 growing to \$2,300 in one year means $r = \dfrac{2300}{2000} - 1 = 0.15 = 15\%$
Moves that are not allowed

The statements below look like shortcuts but are false, and each one generates a standard wrong answer. Keep them in mind.

Case 1not allowed:   $(1+r)^{\,t} \neq 1 + rt$   for $t > 1$
Compound and simple agree only at exactly one year. At 10% for 2 years: compound gives $\times 1.21$, simple gives $\times 1.20$ — and the gap widens every year after.
Case 2not allowed:   up $10\%$ then down $10\%$ $\neq$ back to the start
Start at 100. Up 10% → 110; then down 10% takes off 11 → 99. Now reverse the order: down 10% → 90; then up 10% adds only 9 → 99 again. Both orders land below the start — the decrease acts on the larger number, and the increase acts on the smaller one. In multipliers: $\times 1.10 \times 0.90 = \times 0.90 \times 1.10 = \times 0.99$.
Case 3not allowed:   successive percents do not add
Up 30% then up 20% is $\times 1.30 \times 1.20 = \times 1.56$ — a 56% increase, not 50%.
Case 4not allowed:   $r\%$ compounded semiannually $\neq$ $r\%$ applied twice
The per-period rate is $\tfrac{r}{n}$: 8% semiannual means $\times(1.04)^2$, never $\times(1.08)^2$. Splitting the year splits the rate.
Case 5not allowed:   equal interest earned $\neq$ equal principal
If two accounts at different rates earn the same interest, the principals sit in the inverse ratio of the rates — the higher rate needs less money. Rates 5% and 3% force principals in the ratio $3 : 5$.
