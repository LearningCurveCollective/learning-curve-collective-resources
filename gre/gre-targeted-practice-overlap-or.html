Formulas for overlap and “or”

Two groups

Total $=$ A $+$ B $-$ Both $+$ Neither

Adding A and B counts the people in both groups twice, so one copy of “Both” comes out.

50 people: 30 drink tea, 28 drink coffee, and 4 drink neither. Both ${= 30 + 28 + 4 - 50}$, which is 12.

The pieces

Only A $=$ A $-$ Both

Exactly one $=$ A $+$ B $-$ 2 $\times$ Both

At least one $=$ A $+$ B $-$ Both

Neither $=$ Total $-$ (at least one)

“Or” in probability

$P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)$

“Or” includes the case in which both happen, unless the question says “but not both.”

If A and B cannot both happen, $P(A \text{ and } B) = 0$.

“Or” from a table

Add the row total and the column total, then subtract the cell where they cross. That cell is inside both totals.

(row total $+$ column total $-$ shared cell) $\div$ grand total

Independent events

$P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A) \times P(B)$

$P(\text{neither}) = (1 - P(A))(1 - P(B))$, and $P(\text{at least one}) = 1 - P(\text{neither})$.

Use these when the question says the events are independent.

When the overlap is not given

Both is at least A $+$ B $-$ Total (or 0, if that is negative) and at most the smaller of A and B.

Total 50, A $= 35$, B $= 30$: Both can be anything from 15 to 30.

Three groups

At least one $=$ A $+$ B $+$ C $-$ (AB $+$ BC $+$ AC) $+$ ABC

AB is the number in both A and B, and it includes the people in all three. Or draw three circles and fill them from the center outward: all three, then each pair, then each single group.

“Of the …” names the denominator

“Of the employees who work remotely, what fraction are in Sales?” divides by the number of remote employees, not by the number of all employees.

What to check before you move on

  • Did I subtract the overlap, and only once?
  • Is my count larger than the total, or my probability larger than 1? Then something was counted twice.
  • Which piece does the question ask for: both, only one group, exactly one, at least one, or neither?
  • Which total follows the word “of”?

Numeric Entry

In a group of 80 travelers, 46 have visited Peru, 38 have visited Chile, and 12 have visited neither country. How many of the travelers have visited both countries?

Enter your answer as an integer.

Answer 16

Givens 80 travelers;  Peru 46; Chile 38; neither 12

Plan Use Total $=$ A $+$ B $-$ Both $+$ Neither, and solve for Both.

Steps

  1. $80 = 46 + 38 - \text{Both} + 12$.
  2. $80 = 96 - \text{Both}$.
  3. Both $= 16$.
Check. Only Peru: $46 - 16 = 30$. Only Chile: $38 - 16 = 22$. Then $30 + 22 + 16 + 12 = 80$. ✓
Common mistake. Leaving out the 12 travelers who visited neither country: $46 + 38 - 80 = 4$. The 80 includes those 12, so they belong in the equation.

For events $A$ and $B$, $P(A) = 0.5$, $P(B) = 0.3$, and $P(A \text{ and } B) = 0.1$. What is $P(A \text{ or } B)$?

Answer B (0.7)

Givens $P(A) = 0.5$; $P(B) = 0.3$; $P(A \text{ and } B) = 0.1$

Plan Add the two probabilities and subtract the overlap once.

Steps

  1. $P(A \text{ or } B) = 0.5 + 0.3 - 0.1 = 0.7$.
Check. By pieces: only $A$ is $0.5 - 0.1 = 0.4$, only $B$ is $0.3 - 0.1 = 0.2$, and both is $0.1$. $0.4 + 0.2 + 0.1 = 0.7$. ✓
Common mistake. Adding the overlap to get 0.9, or leaving it in twice to get 0.8. The 0.1 is already inside both the 0.5 and the 0.3.

Numeric Entry

Of the 60 students in a program, 35 play an instrument, 30 play a sport, and 15 do both. How many of the students do exactly one of the two activities?

Enter your answer as an integer.

Answer 35

Givens 60 students;  Instrument 35; sport 30; both 15

Plan Take the students who do both out of each group, then add what is left.

Steps

  1. Instrument only: $35 - 15 = 20$.
  2. Sport only: $30 - 15 = 15$.
  3. Exactly one: $20 + 15 = 35$.
Check. $20 + 15 + 15 = 50$ students do at least one activity, and $60 - 50 = 10$ do neither. ✓
Common mistake. Answering 50, the number who do at least one. “Exactly one” leaves out the 15 who do both, so they come out of both groups: $35 + 30 - 2(15)$.

If one of the 200 orders in the table is selected at random, what is the probability that it was for a hot drink or a large drink, or both?

Answer C (0.79)

Givens Hot: 80 orders; large: 110 orders;  Hot and large: 32 orders;  Total: 200 orders

Plan Add the hot total and the large total, then subtract the hot large orders, which are inside both totals.

Steps

  1. $80 + 110 - 32 = 158$.
  2. $\dfrac{158}{200} = 0.79$.
Check. The orders that are neither hot nor large are the 42 small iced drinks, and $200 - 42 = 158$. ✓
Common mistake. Using $\dfrac{80 + 110}{200} = 0.95$, which counts the 32 large hot drinks twice. Adding the 32 would give a probability above 1, a sign that something was counted twice.

Plan “Of the iced drinks” names the group, so the iced total is the denominator.

Steps

Numeric Entry

How many of the integers from 1 to 100, inclusive, are multiples of 4 or multiples of 6?

Enter your answer as an integer.

Answer 33

Givens Integers from 1 to 100;  Multiples of 4 or multiples of 6

Plan Count the multiples of 4, add the multiples of 6, and subtract the integers that are multiples of both.

Steps

  1. Multiples of 4: $100 \div 4 = 25$.
  2. Multiples of 6: $100 \div 6 = 16$ with a remainder, so 16.
  3. Multiples of both 4 and 6 are multiples of 12: $100 \div 12 = 8$ with a remainder, so 8.
  4. $25 + 16 - 8 = 33$.
Check. The multiples of 12 up to 100 are 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84, 96. Each of these 8 integers is in both lists. ✓
Common mistake. Subtracting the multiples of 24, because $4 \times 6 = 24$. An integer is a multiple of both 4 and 6 when it is a multiple of their least common multiple, which is 12.

7
Quantitative Comparison
In a class of 40 students, 28 study Spanish and 25 study French.
Quantity A
The number of students in the class who study neither language
Quantity B
$13$

Answer B (Quantity B is greater)

Givens 40 students; 28 study Spanish; 25 study French;  Quantity A: the number who study neither; Quantity B: $13$

Plan Write “neither” in terms of “both,” then use the largest value that “both” can take.

Steps

  1. At least one language: $28 + 25 - \text{Both} = 53 - \text{Both}$.
  2. Neither: $40 - (53 - \text{Both}) = \text{Both} - 13$.
  3. Both can be at most 25, the size of the smaller group.
  4. So Neither is at most $25 - 13 = 12$, and $12 < 13$. Quantity B is greater.
Check. If all 25 French students also study Spanish, then 28 students study a language and 12 study neither. If Both is 13, nobody studies neither. ✓
Common mistake. Choosing D because the overlap is not given. The overlap has a largest possible value, and that puts a ceiling of 12 on Quantity A.

Select one

Of the 150 guests at a resort, 80 swam, 70 played tennis, and 60 took a cooking class. Of these guests, 30 both swam and played tennis, 25 both played tennis and took the class, 20 both swam and took the class, and 10 did all three. How many of the guests did exactly one of the three activities?

Answer B (90)

Givens 150 guests;  Swam 80; tennis 70; class 60;  Swam and tennis 30; tennis and class 25; swam and class 20; all three 10

Plan Fill a three-circle diagram from the center outward: all three, then each pair, then each single activity.

Steps

  1. All three: 10.
  2. Exactly two: $30 - 10 = 20$, $25 - 10 = 15$ and $20 - 10 = 10$.
  3. Swam only: $80 - 20 - 10 - 10 = 40$. Tennis only: $70 - 20 - 15 - 10 = 25$. Class only: $60 - 15 - 10 - 10 = 25$.
  4. Exactly one: $40 + 25 + 25 = 90$.
Check. At least one: $90 + 45 + 10 = 145$, which matches $80 + 70 + 60 - (30 + 25 + 20) + 10 = 145$. Five guests did none of the three. ✓
Common mistake. Reporting 145, the number who did at least one activity, or 45, the number who did exactly two. Check which region the question asks for.

If one of the 240 animals in the table is selected at random, what is the probability that it is a cat or is under 2 years old, or both?

Answer D (0.725)

Givens Cats: 120; under 2 years old: 126;  Cats under 2 years old: 72;  Total: 240

Plan Add the cat total and the under-2 total, then subtract the cats under 2.

Steps

  1. $120 + 126 - 72 = 174$.
  2. $\dfrac{174}{240} = 0.725$.
Check. The animals that are neither cats nor under 2 are the 66 older dogs, and $240 - 66 = 174$. ✓
Common mistake. Adding 120 and 126 without subtracting. That gives 246, which is more than the 240 animals at the shelter, so something has been counted twice.

Numeric Entry

A building has two smoke alarms that work independently of each other. When there is smoke, the first alarm sounds with probability 0.9 and the second alarm sounds with probability 0.8. What is the probability that at least one of the alarms sounds when there is smoke?

Enter your answer as a decimal.

Answer 0.98

Givens The alarms are independent;  First alarm sounds with probability 0.9; second with probability 0.8

Plan “At least one” is 1 minus the probability that neither alarm sounds.

Steps

  1. Neither sounds: $(1 - 0.9)(1 - 0.8) = 0.1 \times 0.2 = 0.02$.
  2. At least one sounds: $1 - 0.02 = 0.98$.
Check. With the “or” formula: $0.9 + 0.8 - (0.9)(0.8) = 1.7 - 0.72 = 0.98$. ✓
Common mistake. Adding to get 1.7, which cannot be a probability, or multiplying to get 0.72, which is the probability that both alarms sound.

Select one or more

For events $A$ and $B$, $P(A) = 0.6$ and $P(B) = 0.7$. Which of the following could be the value of $P(A \text{ and } B)$?

Indicate all such values.

Answer B, C, D, E

Givens $P(A) = 0.6$ and $P(B) = 0.7$;  Nothing says the events are independent

Plan Find the smallest and the largest overlap the two probabilities allow.

Steps

  1. $P(A \text{ or } B)$ cannot be more than 1, so $0.6 + 0.7 - P(A \text{ and } B) \le 1$, which gives $P(A \text{ and } B) \ge 0.3$.
  2. The overlap cannot be larger than the smaller probability, so $P(A \text{ and } B) \le 0.6$.
  3. Values from 0.3 through 0.6 work: 0.3, 0.42, 0.55 and 0.6.
Check. 0.42 is $0.6 \times 0.7$, the value when the events are independent. 0.6 happens when $A$ occurs only together with $B$. ✓
Common mistake. Selecting only 0.42. Multiplying the probabilities applies when the events are independent, and the question does not say that they are.

Quantitative Comparison
Events $E$ and $F$ are independent. $P(E) = 0.4$ and $P(F) = 0.25$.
Quantity A
$P(E \text{ or } F)$
Quantity B
$0.55$

Answer C (The two quantities are equal)

Givens $E$ and $F$ are independent;  $P(E) = 0.4$ and $P(F) = 0.25$;  Quantity A: $P(E \text{ or } F)$; Quantity B: $0.55$

Plan For independent events the overlap is the product. Then use the “or” formula.

Steps

  1. $P(E \text{ and } F) = 0.4 \times 0.25 = 0.1$.
  2. $P(E \text{ or } F) = 0.4 + 0.25 - 0.1 = 0.55$.
  3. The quantities are equal.
Check. $1 - (0.6)(0.75) = 1 - 0.45 = 0.55$. ✓
Common mistake. Adding 0.4 and 0.25 to get 0.65 and choosing A. The two events can happen together, so the overlap of 0.1 has to come out.
