Factoring Patterns

Step 0 — GCF first, always

$$ab + ac = a(b + c)$$

Pull out the greatest common factor before looking for any pattern. $3x^3 - 12x = 3x(x^2-4) = 3x(x-2)(x+2)$.

Difference of Squares

$$a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$$

Two perfect squares, subtracted. A sum of squares $a^2+b^2$ does not factor over the reals.

Difference of Cubes

$$a^3 - b^3 = (a-b)(a^2 + ab + b^2)$$

Short factor keeps the sign; long factor flips it. The trinomial does not factor further.

Sum of Cubes

$$a^3 + b^3 = (a+b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$$

SOAP: Same sign, Opposite sign, Always Positive.

Factoring by Grouping (4 terms)

$$ax + ay + bx + by = a(x+y) + b(x+y) = (a+b)(x+y)$$

Factor each pair; if the leftover binomials match, pull it out. Check whether the result factors again.

AC Method (trinomial, $a \ne 1$)

$$ax^2 + bx + c \;\to\; \text{find } m,n:\; mn = ac,\; m+n = b$$

Split $bx$ into $mx + nx$, then factor by grouping. Turns any trinomial into a grouping problem.

Perfect Square Trinomials

$$a^2 \pm 2ab + b^2 = (a \pm b)^2$$

Check: are the outer terms squares, and is the middle term twice their product?

Nested patterns

$$x^4 - 16 = (x^2-4)(x^2+4) = (x-2)(x+2)(x^2+4)$$

"Completely factored" means keep going until nothing else factors.

Splitting & Simplifying Fractions

Split over a monomial denominator

$$\frac{a + b - c}{d} = \frac{a}{d} + \frac{b}{d} - \frac{c}{d}$$

Every term in the numerator gets its own copy of the denominator.

Rewrite as powers of $x$

$$\frac{c}{x^n} = c\,x^{-n} \qquad \sqrt[m]{x^n} = x^{n/m}$$

The end goal is a sum of $c\,x^{p}$ terms — the form calculus wants.

Simplify by factoring

$$\frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 5x + 6} = \frac{(x-3)\cancel{(x+3)}}{(x+2)\cancel{(x+3)}} = \frac{x-3}{x+2}$$

Factor top and bottom fully, cancel common factors, note the restriction ($x \ne -3$).

Combine back (common denominator)

$$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad + bc}{bd}$$

Splitting in reverse. Use the LCD when the denominators share factors.

Two illegal moves.

You can split the numerator, never the denominator: $\dfrac{1}{x+1} \ne \dfrac{1}{x} + 1$.

You cancel factors, never terms: $\dfrac{x^2 + 4}{x} \ne x + 4$. (The $x$ divides $x^2$, but it also has to divide $4$.)

3

Rational & Radical Expressions

Add / subtract with an LCD

$$\frac{3}{x-2} + \frac{2}{x+1} = \frac{3(x+1) + 2(x-2)}{(x-2)(x+1)}$$

Factor every denominator first. The LCD is each distinct factor to its highest power — don't just multiply them all.

Complex fraction: clear with the LCD

$$\frac{\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y}}{\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{y}} \cdot \frac{xy}{xy} = \frac{y + x}{y - x}$$

Multiply top and bottom by the LCD of every small fraction. One step, no stacking.

Conjugates

$$(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}) = a - b$$

Multiply by the conjugate over itself to rationalize a denominator — or a numerator (the limit trick).

Rationalize a numerator

$$\frac{\sqrt{x+h} - \sqrt{x}}{h} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}}$$

The $h$ in the numerator appears after the conjugate multiply and cancels. This is the whole point.

4

Exponent Rules

Product & Quotient

$$x^m \cdot x^n = x^{m+n} \qquad \frac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$$

Same base required. $x^2 \cdot y^3$ cannot be combined.

Power rules

$$(x^m)^n = x^{mn} \qquad (xy)^n = x^n y^n \qquad \left(\tfrac{x}{y}\right)^n = \tfrac{x^n}{y^n}$$

Powers distribute over multiplication and division — never over $+$ or $-$.

Zero & Negative

$$x^0 = 1 \qquad x^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n} \qquad \frac{1}{x^{-n}} = x^n$$

A negative exponent means "flip it" — it does not make the value negative.

Rational exponents

$$x^{m/n} = \sqrt[n]{x^m} = \left(\sqrt[n]{x}\right)^m$$

Root first, then power, for numbers: $27^{2/3} = (\sqrt[3]{27})^2 = 3^2 = 9$.

Same-base equations

$$b^{u} = b^{v} \;\Rightarrow\; u = v \quad (b > 0,\ b \ne 1)$$

Rewrite both sides with one base ($4 = 2^2$, $8 = 2^3$, $\tfrac19 = 3^{-2}$), then set exponents equal.

Factor out the lowest power

$$x^{-2} + x^{1} = x^{-2}\left(1 + x^{3}\right)$$

Pull out the smallest (most negative) exponent; subtract it from each term. Standard calc-cleanup move.

5

Calc-Prep Moves

Difference quotient

$$\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$

Expand $f(x+h)$, subtract all of $f(x)$, factor $h$ out of what's left, cancel. Every term without an $h$ must vanish.

Completing the square

$$x^2 + bx = \left(x + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2$$

Half the middle coefficient, square it, add and subtract. If $a \ne 1$, factor $a$ out of the $x$-terms first.

Synthetic division by $x - k$

$$\text{bring down, } \times k, \text{ add, repeat}$$

Use $k$ (the root), not $-k$. Fill missing powers with $0$. Last number is the remainder; the rest are the quotient's coefficients, one degree lower.

Remainder & Factor Theorems

$$f(x) \div (x - k) \text{ leaves remainder } f(k)$$

So $f(k) = 0 \iff (x - k)$ is a factor. Fast way to check a candidate root.

6

Which Factoring Move?

1. GCF? Pull it out. Then look at what's left.
2. Two terms? Difference of squares $\to$ $(a-b)(a+b)$. Difference / sum of cubes $\to$ SOAP. Sum of squares $\to$ stop.
3. Three terms? Perfect-square check first. Otherwise $a = 1$: find two numbers with product $c$, sum $b$. $a \ne 1$: AC method, then group.
4. Four terms? Group in pairs (try reordering if the first pairing fails).
5. Done? Look at each factor again. A leftover $x^2 - 9$ or $x^3 + 8$ still needs factoring.
1

Difference of Squares

$a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$

Concept

Identify $a$ and $b$ by taking the square root of each term. Coefficients and variables both count: $9x^2 = (3x)^2$, $25y^2 = (5y)^2$, $x^4 = (x^2)^2$.

Worked Example

Factor completely: $\;9x^2 - 25y^2$  and  $x^4 - 16$

Identify $a$ and $b$$a = 3x,\; b = 5y$
Apply the pattern$$9x^2 - 25y^2 = (3x - 5y)(3x + 5y)$$
Identify $a$ and $b$$a = x^2,\; b = 4$
Apply the pattern$x^4 - 16 = (x^2 - 4)(x^2 + 4)$
Check each factor again — $x^2 - 4$ is another difference of squares$$\boxed{(x-2)(x+2)(x^2+4)}$$

$x^2 + 4$ is a sum of squares — it stays.

Practice

Factor completely: $\;(x+1)^2 - 9$

$a = x+1,\; b = 3$. So $(x+1-3)(x+1+3) = \boxed{(x-2)(x+4)}$.

Sum & Difference of Cubes

$a^3 \pm b^3 = (a \pm b)(a^2 \mp ab + b^2)$

Concept

Take the cube root of each term to get $a$ and $b$. Then write the two factors using SOAP: the binomial has the Same sign as the original, the middle term of the trinomial has the Opposite sign, and the last term is Always Positive.

Perfect cubes to recognize on sight: $1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1000$ and $x^3, x^6, x^9$.
Worked Example

Factor: $\;8x^3 - 125$

Cube roots$a = \sqrt[3]{8x^3} = 2x,\quad b = \sqrt[3]{125} = 5$
Binomial: Same sign$(2x - 5)$
Trinomial: $a^2$, Opposite sign on $ab$, $b^2$ Always Positive$(2x)^2 + (2x)(5) + 5^2 = 4x^2 + 10x + 25$
Result$$\boxed{(2x-5)(4x^2+10x+25)}$$

Quick check: FOIL-expanding, the middle terms $10x^2 \cdot 2x$-type products cancel, leaving $8x^3 - 125$. ✓

Practice

Factor completely: $\;2x^3 + 54$

GCF first: $2(x^3 + 27)$. Then sum of cubes with $a = x, b = 3$: $\boxed{2(x+3)(x^2 - 3x + 9)}$.

Factoring by Grouping

Four terms: pair, factor, match

Concept

Split the four terms into two pairs. Factor the GCF out of each pair. If both pairs leave the same binomial, factor that binomial out. If they don't match, try a different pairing — or check whether a minus sign needs to be factored out of the second pair.

Worked Example

Factor completely: $\;x^3 + 2x^2 - 9x - 18$

Pair the terms$(x^3 + 2x^2) + (-9x - 18)$
Factor each pair (note the $-9$, so the binomial matches)$x^2(x + 2) - 9(x + 2)$
Common binomial $\to$ factor it out$(x+2)(x^2 - 9)$
Keep going: $x^2 - 9$ is a difference of squares$$\boxed{(x+2)(x-3)(x+3)}$$
Worked Example — AC method

Factor: $\;6x^2 + 7x - 20$

$a = 6,\; b = 7,\; c = -20$$ac = -120$
Find $m, n$ with $mn = -120$ and $m + n = 7$$m = 15,\; n = -8$
Split the middle term$6x^2 + 15x - 8x - 20$
Group and factor each pair$3x(2x + 5) - 4(2x + 5)$
Factor out the common binomial$$\boxed{(3x-4)(2x+5)}$$
Practice

Factor completely: $\;2x^3 - 3x^2 + 8x - 12$

$x^2(2x - 3) + 4(2x - 3) = \boxed{(2x-3)(x^2+4)}$. The $x^2 + 4$ is a sum of squares — done.

Common slips.

  • Forgetting the GCF, then getting a "wrong" answer that is actually just not finished: $3x^3 - 12x \to 3x(x-2)(x+2)$, not $(3x^2)(x-2)(x+2)$-ish leftovers.
  • Writing $a^3 - b^3 = (a - b)^3$ or $(a-b)(a^2 - ab + b^2)$. The middle sign is opposite the binomial's sign.
  • Trying to factor $x^2 + 9$ as $(x+3)(x-3)$ — that expands to $x^2 - 9$.

Split the Numerator

$\dfrac{a + b}{c} = \dfrac{a}{c} + \dfrac{b}{c}$

Concept

When the denominator is a single term (a monomial), every term in the numerator can be written over its own copy of that denominator. Then reduce each piece separately using the quotient rule $\frac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$.

Worked Example

Rewrite as a sum of terms: $\;\dfrac{4x^3 - 6x^2 + 2x}{2x^2}$

One denominator per term$\dfrac{4x^3}{2x^2} - \dfrac{6x^2}{2x^2} + \dfrac{2x}{2x^2}$
Reduce coefficients, subtract exponents$2x^{1} - 3x^{0} + x^{-1}$
Clean up$$\boxed{2x - 3 + \frac{1}{x}}$$
Practice

Rewrite as a sum of terms: $\;\dfrac{x^2 + 3x - 5}{x}$

$\dfrac{x^2}{x} + \dfrac{3x}{x} - \dfrac{5}{x} = \boxed{x + 3 - \dfrac{5}{x}}$

Convert to $c\,x^{p}$ Form

Negative and fractional exponents replace fractions and radicals

Concept

Calculus (and a lot of precalc simplification) wants every term shaped like coefficient × $x$ to a power. Two conversions do all the work:

$$\frac{c}{x^n} = c\,x^{-n} \qquad\qquad \sqrt[m]{x^n} = x^{n/m}$$

Only the thing being divided or rooted gets the negative/fractional exponent. In $\dfrac{3}{x^4}$, the $3$ stays a $3$: $3x^{-4}$, not $(3x)^{-4}$.

Worked Example

Rewrite with no fractions or radicals: $\;\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{\sqrt{x}}$

Rewrite the radical$\sqrt{x} = x^{1/2}$
Split the numerator$\dfrac{x^2}{x^{1/2}} + \dfrac{1}{x^{1/2}}$
Quotient rule: $2 - \tfrac12 = \tfrac32$; and $\tfrac{1}{x^{1/2}} = x^{-1/2}$$$\boxed{x^{3/2} + x^{-1/2}}$$
Practice

Rewrite with no fractions or radicals: $\;\dfrac{3}{x^4} - \dfrac{2}{\sqrt[3]{x}}$

$\boxed{3x^{-4} - 2x^{-1/3}}$

Simplify by Factoring

Factor top and bottom, cancel common factors

Concept

When the denominator is not a monomial, you can't split. Instead, factor numerator and denominator completely and cancel any factor that appears in both. Record the restriction from the cancelled factor — the original expression was undefined there.

Worked Example

Simplify: $\;\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 + 5x + 6}$

Factor numerator (difference of squares)$(x-3)(x+3)$
Factor denominator (product $6$, sum $5$)$(x+2)(x+3)$
Cancel the shared factor$$\boxed{\frac{x-3}{x+2}},\quad x \ne -3$$
Practice

Simplify: $\;\dfrac{x^3 + 8}{x^2 - 4}$

$\dfrac{(x+2)(x^2 - 2x + 4)}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \boxed{\dfrac{x^2 - 2x + 4}{x - 2}},\quad x \ne -2$

The number-one error on this topic: cancelling a term instead of a factor.

$\dfrac{x + 4}{x} \ne 4$   and   $\dfrac{x^2 + 4}{x} \ne x + 4$.   Correct: $\dfrac{x^2 + 4}{x} = x + \dfrac{4}{x} = x + 4x^{-1}$.

Test it with a number: at $x = 2$, $\dfrac{2^2 + 4}{2} = 4$, but $2 + 4 = 6$. If plugging in a value breaks your "simplification," it was never equal.

Simplifying with the Rules

Product, quotient, power, negative

Concept

Work outside-in: distribute any outer power onto every factor inside first, then combine like bases by adding/subtracting exponents, then move negative exponents across the fraction bar at the very end.

$$x^m x^n = x^{m+n} \qquad \frac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n} \qquad (x^m)^n = x^{mn} \qquad (xy)^n = x^n y^n \qquad x^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n}$$
Worked Example

Simplify, positive exponents only: $\;\left(2x^3y^{-2}\right)^3$  and  $\left(\dfrac{4x^2}{y^{-3}}\right)^{-2}$

Distribute the outer power to each factor$2^3 \, x^{3\cdot3} \, y^{-2\cdot3} = 8x^9 y^{-6}$
Move the negative exponent$$\boxed{\frac{8x^9}{y^6}}$$
Clean the inside first: $\frac{1}{y^{-3}} = y^3$$\left(4x^2y^3\right)^{-2}$
Distribute $-2$$4^{-2} x^{-4} y^{-6}$
Flip everything with a negative exponent$$\boxed{\frac{1}{16x^4y^6}}$$
Practice

Simplify: $\;\dfrac{x^5 \cdot x^{-2}}{x^4}$

$x^{5 + (-2) - 4} = x^{-1} = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{x}}$

Rational Exponents & Radicals

$x^{m/n} = \left(\sqrt[n]{x}\right)^m$

Concept

The denominator of the exponent is the root; the numerator is the power. With numbers, take the root first to keep the arithmetic small. A negative rational exponent means root, power, then flip.

Worked Example

Evaluate: $\;27^{2/3}$, $\;16^{-3/4}$.   Simplify: $\;\sqrt{x^3}\cdot\sqrt[3]{x}$

Root first, then power$27^{2/3} = \left(\sqrt[3]{27}\right)^2 = 3^2 = \boxed{9}$
Root, power, flip$16^{-3/4} = \dfrac{1}{\left(\sqrt[4]{16}\right)^3} = \dfrac{1}{2^3} = \boxed{\dfrac{1}{8}}$
Convert radicals to exponents, then add$x^{3/2}\cdot x^{1/3} = x^{\frac{9}{6} + \frac{2}{6}} = \boxed{x^{11/6}}$
Practice

Evaluate $\;8^{4/3}$  and  $(-32)^{3/5}$

$8^{4/3} = (\sqrt[3]{8})^4 = 2^4 = \boxed{16}$.   $(-32)^{3/5} = (\sqrt[5]{-32})^3 = (-2)^3 = \boxed{-8}$ (odd roots of negatives are fine).

Same-Base Exponential Equations

If $b^u = b^v$ then $u = v$

Concept

Rewrite both sides as powers of the same base, then equate the exponents and solve the resulting linear equation. Useful base facts: $4, 8, 16, 32 = 2^2, 2^3, 2^4, 2^5$; $9, 27, 81 = 3^2, 3^3, 3^4$; $\tfrac{1}{b^n} = b^{-n}$.

Worked Example

Solve: $\;9^x = 27$

Common base $3$$(3^2)^x = 3^3$
Power rule$3^{2x} = 3^3$
Equate exponents$2x = 3 \;\Rightarrow\; \boxed{x = \tfrac{3}{2}}$
Practice

Solve: $\;2^{x+1} = 32$

$2^{x+1} = 2^5 \Rightarrow x + 1 = 5 \Rightarrow \boxed{x = 4}$

Factoring Out the Lowest Power

The GCF can have a negative or fractional exponent

Concept

When terms share a base with different exponents, factor out the smallest exponent. Inside the parentheses, each term's exponent is (original) − (the one you pulled out). This is how derivatives get cleaned up later, so it's worth making automatic now.

Worked Example

Factor: $\;3x^{-2} + 6x^{-1}$  and  $x^{1/2} + x^{3/2}$

Lowest power is $x^{-2}$; numeric GCF is $3$$3x^{-2}(\;?\;)$
Subtract exponents: $-2-(-2) = 0$, $-1-(-2) = 1$$$\boxed{3x^{-2}(1 + 2x)}$$
Lowest power is $x^{1/2}$$x^{1/2}(\;?\;)$
Subtract: $\tfrac12 - \tfrac12 = 0$, $\tfrac32 - \tfrac12 = 1$$$\boxed{x^{1/2}(1 + x)}$$

Check by distributing: $x^{1/2}\cdot x = x^{3/2}$. ✓

Practice

Factor: $\;4x^{1/2} - 2x^{-1/2}$

Lowest power $x^{-1/2}$, numeric GCF $2$: $\boxed{2x^{-1/2}(2x - 1)}$

Watch for these.

  • $(x + y)^2 \ne x^2 + y^2$. Powers don't distribute over addition.
  • $x^{-2} \ne -x^2$. Negative exponent = reciprocal, not negative value.
  • $2^3 \cdot 2^4 = 2^7$, not $4^7$. The base stays put; only exponents add.
  • $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$ in general. On this sheet, assume variables are positive unless told otherwise.

Combining with an LCD

Factor denominators, build the LCD, rewrite, combine

Concept

Factor every denominator completely. The LCD contains each distinct factor to the highest power it appears anywhere. Multiply each fraction's top and bottom by whatever its denominator is missing, then add the numerators over the single LCD. Simplify the numerator, then check whether it factors and cancels.

Worked Example

Combine: $\;\dfrac{x}{x^2 - 4} - \dfrac{1}{x+2}$

Factor denominators$\dfrac{x}{(x-2)(x+2)} - \dfrac{1}{x+2}$
LCD is $(x-2)(x+2)$; second fraction is missing $(x-2)$$\dfrac{x}{(x-2)(x+2)} - \dfrac{1\cdot(x-2)}{(x+2)(x-2)}$
Combine numerators — distribute the minus sign$\dfrac{x - (x - 2)}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \dfrac{x - x + 2}{(x-2)(x+2)}$
Simplify$$\boxed{\frac{2}{(x-2)(x+2)}}$$
Practice

Combine: $\;\dfrac{3}{x-2} + \dfrac{2}{x+1}$

$\dfrac{3(x+1) + 2(x-2)}{(x-2)(x+1)} = \dfrac{3x + 3 + 2x - 4}{(x-2)(x+1)} = \boxed{\dfrac{5x - 1}{(x-2)(x+1)}}$

Complex Fractions

Multiply top and bottom by the LCD of every small fraction

Concept

A fraction with fractions inside it. Find the LCD of all the little denominators (top and bottom together), multiply the big numerator and big denominator by that LCD, and every small fraction clears at once. Then factor and cancel as usual.

Worked Example

Simplify: $\;\dfrac{1 - \frac{4}{x^2}}{1 + \frac{2}{x}}$

Little denominators: $x^2$ and $x$. LCD $= x^2$$\dfrac{\left(1 - \frac{4}{x^2}\right)\cdot x^2}{\left(1 + \frac{2}{x}\right)\cdot x^2}$
Distribute — every small fraction clears$\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 + 2x}$
Factor and cancel$\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x(x+2)} = \boxed{\dfrac{x-2}{x}}$
Practice

Simplify: $\;\dfrac{\frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{y}}{\frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{y}}$

Multiply by $\dfrac{xy}{xy}$: $\dfrac{y + x}{y - x} = \boxed{\dfrac{x+y}{y-x}}$

Rationalizing with Conjugates

$(\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b})(\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}) = a - b$

Concept

The conjugate of $\sqrt{a} - \sqrt{b}$ is $\sqrt{a} + \sqrt{b}$. Multiplying them is a difference of squares, and the radicals disappear. Multiply the fraction by $\dfrac{\text{conjugate}}{\text{conjugate}}$ — it's just multiplying by $1$. Rationalizing the numerator uses the identical move and is what makes limits work later.

Worked Example — denominator

Rationalize: $\;\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{2}}$

Multiply by the conjugate over itself$\dfrac{6}{\sqrt5 - \sqrt2}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt5 + \sqrt2}{\sqrt5 + \sqrt2}$
Denominator becomes $a - b$$\dfrac{6(\sqrt5 + \sqrt2)}{5 - 2} = \dfrac{6(\sqrt5 + \sqrt2)}{3}$
Reduce$$\boxed{2\sqrt5 + 2\sqrt2}$$
Worked Example — numerator

Rationalize the numerator: $\;\dfrac{\sqrt{x+h} - \sqrt{x}}{h}$

Multiply by the conjugate of the numerator$\dfrac{\sqrt{x+h} - \sqrt{x}}{h}\cdot\dfrac{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}}{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}}$
Numerator becomes $a - b$$\dfrac{(x + h) - x}{h\left(\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}\right)} = \dfrac{h}{h\left(\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}\right)}$
Cancel $h$$$\boxed{\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+h} + \sqrt{x}}}$$

Leave the denominator unexpanded — the whole point was to get rid of the $h$ on top.

Practice

Simplify: $\;\dfrac{x - 4}{\sqrt{x} - 2}$

$\dfrac{(x-4)(\sqrt x + 2)}{(\sqrt x - 2)(\sqrt x + 2)} = \dfrac{(x-4)(\sqrt x + 2)}{x - 4} = \boxed{\sqrt{x} + 2}$

Common slips.

  • Subtracting fractions: the minus sign applies to the entire second numerator. $x - (x - 2) = 2$, not $-2$.
  • Building the LCD by multiplying all denominators together: $\frac{1}{x^2-4} + \frac{1}{x+2}$ has LCD $(x-2)(x+2)$, not $(x^2-4)(x+2)$.
  • Conjugate of $\sqrt{a} + b$ is $\sqrt{a} - b$ — flip only the sign between the two terms, nothing else.

The Difference Quotient

$\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$

Concept

This is the slope of the line through $(x, f(x))$ and $(x+h, f(x+h))$; calculus will send $h \to 0$. Procedure: (1) write $f(x+h)$ by replacing every $x$ with $(x+h)$ and expand; (2) subtract the entire $f(x)$ in parentheses; (3) every term without an $h$ must cancel — if one survives, there's an algebra error; (4) factor $h$ out of the numerator and cancel it.

Worked Example — polynomial

$f(x) = x^2 - 3x$. Simplify $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$.

Build $f(x+h)$$(x+h)^2 - 3(x+h) = x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - 3x - 3h$
Subtract all of $f(x)$$x^2 + 2xh + h^2 - 3x - 3h - (x^2 - 3x)$
The $h$-free terms cancel$2xh + h^2 - 3h$
Factor out $h$, divide$\dfrac{h(2x + h - 3)}{h} = \boxed{2x + h - 3}$
Worked Example — rational

$f(x) = \dfrac{1}{x}$. Simplify $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$.

Set up — a complex fraction$\dfrac{\frac{1}{x+h} - \frac{1}{x}}{h}$
Combine the top over LCD $x(x+h)$$\dfrac{\frac{x - (x+h)}{x(x+h)}}{h} = \dfrac{\frac{-h}{x(x+h)}}{h}$
Dividing by $h$ = multiplying by $\frac1h$; cancel $h$$$\boxed{\frac{-1}{x(x+h)}}$$
Practice

$f(x) = 2x^2 - x$. Simplify $\dfrac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$.

$\dfrac{2(x+h)^2 - (x+h) - 2x^2 + x}{h} = \dfrac{4xh + 2h^2 - h}{h} = \boxed{4x + 2h - 1}$

Completing the Square

$x^2 + bx + c = \left(x + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 + c$

Concept

Take half the $x$-coefficient, square it, add it and subtract it. The first three terms are now a perfect square. If there's a leading coefficient $a \ne 1$, factor $a$ out of the $x^2$ and $x$ terms only, complete the square inside, then remember the subtracted piece gets multiplied by $a$ when it comes back out.

Worked Example

Write in vertex form: $\;2x^2 - 12x + 5$

Factor $a = 2$ out of the $x$-terms$2(x^2 - 6x) + 5$
Half of $-6$ is $-3$; square is $9$. Add and subtract inside$2(x^2 - 6x + 9 - 9) + 5$
Perfect square; the $-9$ leaves the parentheses multiplied by $2$$2(x - 3)^2 - 18 + 5$
Result$$\boxed{2(x-3)^2 - 13}$$

Vertex $(3, -13)$. Check: $2(3)^2 - 12(3) + 5 = 18 - 36 + 5 = -13$. ✓

Worked Example — circle

Find center and radius: $\;x^2 + y^2 - 4x + 6y = 3$

Group by variable$(x^2 - 4x) + (y^2 + 6y) = 3$

Result$(x-2)^2 + (y+3)^2 = 16$   $\Rightarrow$   $\boxed{\text{center } (2,-3),\ r = 4}$

Polynomial Division

Synthetic division for $x - k$; long division for everything else

Concept

Synthetic division only works for a linear divisor $x - k$. Write the coefficients (put $0$ for any missing power), put $k$ in the corner, bring down the first coefficient, then repeat: multiply by $k$, add to the next coefficient. The last number is the remainder $= f(k)$; the others are the quotient, one degree lower.

Long division works for any divisor. Divide leading terms, multiply back, subtract (distribute the minus!), bring down, repeat until the remainder's degree is less than the divisor's.

Worked Example — synthetic

Divide $x^3 - 2x^2 - 5x + 6$ by $x - 1$, then factor completely.

$k = 1$. Coefficients: $1,\ -2,\ -5,\ 6$ $$\begin{array}{c|rrrr} 1 & 1 & -2 & -5 & 6 \\ & & 1 & -1 & -6 \\ \hline & 1 & -1 & -6 & 0 \end{array}$$
Read off: quotient $x^2 - x - 6$, remainder $0$$x^3 - 2x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x-1)(x^2 - x - 6)$
Factor the quotient$$\boxed{(x-1)(x-3)(x+2)}$$

Remainder $0$ confirms $x = 1$ is a root — the Factor Theorem in action.

Worked Example — long division

Divide $2x^3 + 3x^2 - x + 4$ by $x^2 + 1$.

$2x^3 \div x^2 = 2x$. Multiply back, subtract$(2x^3 + 3x^2 - x + 4) - (2x^3 + 2x) = 3x^2 - 3x + 4$
$3x^2 \div x^2 = 3$. Multiply back, subtract$(3x^2 - 3x + 4) - (3x^2 + 3) = -3x + 1$
Degree of $-3x + 1$ is below $2$ — stop$$\boxed{2x + 3 + \frac{-3x + 1}{x^2 + 1}}$$

Quotient $2x + 3$, remainder $-3x + 1$. Check: $(x^2+1)(2x+3) + (-3x+1) = 2x^3 + 3x^2 + 2x + 3 - 3x + 1$. ✓

Practice

Use the Remainder Theorem: what is the remainder when $x^3 - 2x^2 - 5x + 6$ is divided by $x - 2$?

$f(2) = 8 - 8 - 10 + 6 = \boxed{-4}$. No division needed.

Common slips.

  • $f(x+h) \ne f(x) + h$. You replace $x$ with $(x+h)$ everywhere, then expand — $(x+h)^2 = x^2 + 2xh + h^2$.
  • Completing the square with $a \ne 1$: the number you subtract inside the parentheses is worth $a$ times that outside.
  • Synthetic division by $x + 5$ uses $k = -5$. And a missing $x^2$ term still needs its $0$ placeholder.
