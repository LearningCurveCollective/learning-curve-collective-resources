You've put in the work. Here's how to make the most of it on test day.

At this point, the goal isn't to learn anything new. It's to give yourself the best chance to demonstrate what you already know. Over the next two days, we'll focus on a few things that really matter: sleep, some targeted review, getting organized, and having a clear strategy for test day.

Use this checklist to keep yourself on track. You can check things off as you go, and your progress will be saved on this device.

48 Hours BeforeTwo nights before your test

Getting a good night's sleep tonight is especially important. It's normal to feel a little nervous the night before a big test, so we want to make sure you're well rested heading into that final night.

A note on sleep: The goal is to get two good nights of sleep before your test. If you're a little restless the night before, that's okay. Getting plenty of rest tonight will help.

3

Test MorningGive yourself plenty of time

The most important thing this morning is to avoid rushing. You should have everything packed and ready, so all you need to do is get up, eat breakfast, and get to your test centerschool.

One last reminder: You don't need to get every question right to earn a great score. Focus on one question at a time, use the strategies you've practiced, and keep moving.
4

During the TestPutting your strategies to work

The best test-taking strategies are the ones you've practiced enough that they feel natural. Here's what we want you to remember when you're in the room.

  1. 1
    Start each modulesection with a plan. Remind yourself of CLEAR. Take a breath, get focused, and get started. If you're testing on paper, jot your halfway checkpoint at the top of the section, since you'll be working from the room clock.
  2. 2
    Read carefully before you solve. On Reading & Writing, identify what the question is asking before working through the passage. On Math, identify the givens and what you're solving for before jumping into calculations.On English and Reading, identify what the question is asking before working through the passage. On Math, identify the givens and what you're solving for before jumping into calculations. On Science, find the table or graph the question refers to before reading the whole passage.
  3. 3
    Keep an eye on your pacing. Check your time around the halfway point. If you're behind, make an adjustment. You don't need to watch the clock after every question. The ACT moves faster per question than the SAT, so that halfway check matters even more.
  4. 4
    Don't get stuck on one question. If you've spent time on a problem without making progress, choose your best answer, mark it for reviewflag it (or circle the number in your booklet), and move on. Remember that the questions you haven't reached are worth points too.
  5. 5
    Use elimination whenever you can. Even when you aren't sure of the correct answer, you can often identify choices that are clearly wrong. Use that information to improve your odds.
  6. 6
    Answer every question. There's no guessing penalty. Make sure you've selected an answer before moving on, even if you plan to revisit it.
  7. 7
    Use your final minutes wisely. Check that every question has an answer. If you have extra time, return to the questions you marked and focus on the ones where you think another look could make a difference.
  8. 8
    Take advantage of your break. Get up, stretch, drink some water, and have a snack. Try not to spend the break replaying questions from the previous section. Your attention is better spent on what's ahead.

A few SATPSATACT-specific reminders

Adaptive modules
Your performance on Module 1 helps determine the difficulty of Module 2. Take the first module seriously, but remember that careful work matters throughout the test.
Math answers
Pay attention to the required answer format, especially when entering fractions and decimals. Check your calculations and follow Bluebook's answer-entry instructions.
Desmos
Remember that you have a built-in graphing calculator available throughout the Math section. Use it when it makes a problem easier, but don't force it when a simpler method will do.

Same test for everyone
The ACT isn't adaptive. Everyone sees the same questions in the same order, so a tough question early on doesn't change what comes later. Just keep working.
Four choices, nothing to type

Your own calculator

Your Personal Test Day RemindersThe two things I want to remember

Every student has different strengths and different mistakes they're working to avoid. Before test day, take a minute to identify the two reminders that will be most useful to you. One should be about pacing. The other should be about a mistake you've made before and how you're going to prevent it.

Example: I'll check the clock halfway through each modulesection and adjust my pace if needed.

Example: Sometimes I solve for the wrong thing in Math. I'll underline what the question is asking before I start.

Final thought

You've spent time learning the material, practicing strategies, and working through mistakes. Now the goal is to put that preparation to use.

There will probably be a few questions you don't know immediately. That's expected. What matters is how you respond: stay organized, use what you know, make good decisions with your time, and keep going.

Trust your preparation. Focus on the question in front of you.

Good luck!
