Angles, Lines & Triangles

Complementary & Supplementary

$$\text{Comp: } A + B = 90°$$

$$\text{Supp: } A + B = 180°$$

Vertical angles are always equal.

Parallel Lines & Transversals

Alternate interior angles are $$\cong$$

Corresponding angles are equal. Co-interior (same-side) angles are supplementary.

Triangle Area

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$$

Angle sum: $A + B + C = 180°$

Pythagorean Theorem

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

$c$ is always the hypotenuse (longest side, opposite the right angle).

45-45-90 Triangle

$$x,\; x,\; x\sqrt{2}$$

Legs are equal; hypotenuse = leg × √2.

30-60-90 Triangle

$$x,\; x\sqrt{3},\; 2x$$

Short leg opposite 30°, long leg opposite 60°, hypotenuse opposite 90°.

Equilateral Triangle Area

$$A = \frac{s^2\sqrt{3}}{4}$$

All sides = $s$, all angles = 60°. Saves time vs. computing height.

Triangle Inequality

$$|a - b| < c < a + b$$

Any side must be less than the sum and greater than the difference of the other two.

Quadrilaterals & Polygons

Rectangle

$$A = lw$$

$$P = 2l + 2w$$

Also applies to squares where $l = w$.

Parallelogram

$$A = bh$$

Height must be perpendicular to the base — not the slanted side!

Trapezoid Area

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2)h$$

$b_1$ and $b_2$ are the parallel bases.

Trapezoid Midsegment

$$m = \frac{b_1 + b_2}{2}$$

Connects midpoints of the legs. Parallel to both bases. Length = average of the two bases.

Polygon Interior Angle Sum

$$S = (n-2) \cdot 180°$$

Each angle of a regular $n$-gon: $\frac{(n-2)\cdot 180°}{n}$

Polygon Exterior Angle Sum

$$\text{Sum} = 360° \text{ (always)}$$

Each exterior angle of a regular $n$-gon: $\frac{360°}{n}$

Circles

Circle Basics

$$C = 2\pi r = \pi d$$

$$A = \pi r^2$$

Angle Relationships

Central angle $= $ arc

Inscribed angle $= \tfrac{1}{2}$ arc

Tangent line ⊥ radius at point of tangency.

Arc Length

$$s = \frac{\theta}{360°} \cdot 2\pi r$$

$$s = r\theta \quad (\theta \text{ in radians})$$

Fraction of the circumference.

Sector Area

$$A_{\text{sector}} = \frac{\theta}{360°} \cdot \pi r^2$$

$$A_{\text{sector}} = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta \quad (\theta \text{ in radians})$$

Same fraction of the total area as arc length is of circumference.

Equation of a Circle

$$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$$

Center $(h, k)$, radius $r$. Watch the signs — $(x-3)^2$ means $h=3$.

Coordinate Geometry

Distance Formula

$$d = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}$$

It's just the Pythagorean theorem in disguise.

Midpoint Formula

$$M = \left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\; \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$$

Average the x's, average the y's.

Slope

$$m = \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}= \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$$

Line Equations

Slope-intercept: $y = mx + b$

Point-slope: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$

Parallel: $m_1 = m_2$. Perpendicular: $m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$.

For $y = a\sin(bx - c) + d$.

Angle Relationships

Parallel Lines & Transversals

Concept

Key pairs: Vertical angles (∠1 & ∠3, ∠2 & ∠4) are equal. Corresponding angles (∠1 & ∠5, ∠2 & ∠6, etc.) are equal. Alternate interior (∠3 & ∠5, ∠4 & ∠6) are equal. Co-interior / same-side interior (∠3 & ∠6, ∠4 & ∠5) are supplementary (sum to 180°).

Worked Example

Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. If one of the angles formed is $65°$, find all eight angles.

Given: One angle $= 65°$

Step 1: Vertical angle $= 65°$

Step 2: Supplementary angle $= 180° - 65° = 115°$

Step 3: Corresponding angles at the other intersection are equal.

Answer: Four angles are $65°$ and four angles are $115°$.

Practice

(Easy) Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. If ∠1 = 72°, what is ∠5 (its corresponding angle)?

Corresponding angles are equal, so $\angle 5 = 72°$.

(Medium) Lines $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$ are cut by a transversal. If the alternate interior angles are $(3x + 10)°$ and $(5x - 20)°$, find $x$.

Alternate interior angles are equal:

$3x + 10 = 5x - 20$

$30 = 2x$

$x = 15$

(ACT-Hard) In the figure, $\ell_1 \parallel \ell_2$. A transversal creates an angle of $(2x+15)°$ with $\ell_1$. The co-interior angle with $\ell_2$ is $(3x+40)°$. Find the measure of each angle.

Co-interior angles are supplementary:

$(2x+15) + (3x+40) = 180$

$5x + 55 = 180$

$5x = 125 \implies x = 25$

Angles: $2(25)+15 = 65°$ and $3(25)+40 = 115°$ ✓

Special Right Triangles

45-45-90 and 30-60-90

Concept

Common mistake: Mixing up which leg is $x$ and which is $x\sqrt{3}$ in the 30-60-90. Remember: the short leg ($x$) is always opposite the smallest angle (30°).

Worked Example

The hypotenuse of a 30-60-90 triangle is 10. Find the lengths of both legs.

Given: Hypotenuse $= 2x = 10$

Step 1: Solve for $x$: $x = 5$ (short leg, opposite 30°)

Step 2: Long leg $= x\sqrt{3} = 5\sqrt{3}$ (opposite 60°)

Answer: Short leg $= 5$, Long leg $= 5\sqrt{3}$

Practice

(Easy) A 45-45-90 triangle has legs of length 7. What is the hypotenuse?

Hypotenuse $= 7\sqrt{2}$

(Medium) A 30-60-90 triangle has a long leg of $9\sqrt{3}$. Find the short leg and hypotenuse.

Long leg $= x\sqrt{3} = 9\sqrt{3}$, so $x = 9$.

Short leg $= 9$, Hypotenuse $= 2x = 18$.

(ACT-Hard) The diagonal of a square is $12\sqrt{2}$ cm. What is the area of the square?

A square's diagonal creates two 45-45-90 triangles.

Diagonal $= s\sqrt{2} = 12\sqrt{2}$, so $s = 12$.

Area $= s^2 = 144$ cm².

Pythagorean Theorem

Pythagorean Theorem

Concept

For a right triangle with legs $a$, $b$ and hypotenuse $c$:

$$a^2 + b^2 = c^2$$

Common Pythagorean triples: $(3, 4, 5)$, $(5, 12, 13)$, $(8, 15, 17)$, $(7, 24, 25)$ — and any scalar multiples like $(6, 8, 10)$.

Worked Example

A right triangle has legs of 6 and 8. Find the hypotenuse.

$a = 6$, $b = 8$, $c = ?$

$6^2 + 8^2 = c^2$

$36 + 64 = c^2$

$c^2 = 100 \implies c = 10$

(This is the 3-4-5 triple scaled by 2.)

Practice

(Easy) A right triangle has a leg of 5 and hypotenuse of 13. Find the other leg.

$5^2 + b^2 = 13^2$

$25 + b^2 = 169$

$b^2 = 144 \implies b = 12$

(Medium) A right triangle has legs of length $(x+2)$ and $(x+4)$, and a hypotenuse of 10. Find $x$.

$(x+2)^2 + (x+4)^2 = 10^2$

$x^2 + 4x + 4 + x^2 + 8x + 16 = 100$

$2x^2 + 12x + 20 = 100$

$2x^2 + 12x - 80 = 0 \implies x^2 + 6x - 40 = 0$

$(x+10)(x-4) = 0 \implies x = 4$ (reject $x = -10$ since lengths must be positive)

Legs: $6$ and $8$. Check: $6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10^2$ ✓

(ACT-Hard) A 10-foot ladder leans against a wall. Its base is 6 feet from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach?

Ladder = hypotenuse = 10, base = 6, height = ?

$6^2 + h^2 = 10^2$

$36 + h^2 = 100$

$h^2 = 64 \implies h = 8$ feet

Triangle Inequality Theorem

Concept

For any triangle with sides $a$, $b$, and $c$:

$$|a - b| < c < a + b$$

In words: any one side must be less than the sum and greater than the absolute difference of the other two sides.

Quick check: Just test whether the two shorter sides add up to more than the longest side. If not, the triangle is impossible.

Worked Example

Can a triangle have sides of length 5, 8, and 14?

Step 1: Identify the two shorter sides: $5$ and $8$.

Step 2: Add them: $5 + 8 = 13$.

Step 3: Compare to the longest side: $13 < 14$.

Answer: No — the two shorter sides don't sum to more than the longest side, so this triangle is impossible.

Worked Example

Two sides of a triangle are 7 and 11. What is the range of possible lengths for the third side?

Step 1: Difference: $|11 - 7| = 4$

Step 2: Sum: $11 + 7 = 18$

Answer: The third side must satisfy $4 < c < 18$.

Practice

(Easy) Can a triangle have sides 3, 4, and 5?

$3 + 4 = 7 > 5$ ✓

Yes — this is a valid triangle (and a right triangle!).

(Medium) Two sides of a triangle are 9 and 15. Which of the following could be the third side: 5, 6, 24, or 25?

Range: $|15 - 9| < c < 15 + 9$, so $6 < c < 24$.

$c = 5$? No ($5 < 6$). $c = 6$? No (not strictly greater). $c = 24$? No (not strictly less). $c = 25$? No ($25 > 24$).

None of these work! This is a classic ACT trick — read every answer choice carefully.

(ACT-Hard) The lengths of two sides of a triangle are 10 and $x + 3$. If the third side is 8, find all possible values of $x$.

Apply the inequality three ways:

$10 + 8 > x + 3 \implies x < 15$

$10 + (x+3) > 8 \implies x > -5$ (always true for positive $x$)

$8 + (x+3) > 10 \implies x > -1$ (always true for positive $x$)

Also need $x + 3 > |10 - 8| = 2 \implies x > -1$

And $x + 3 > 0 \implies x > -3$

Answer: Since side lengths must be positive, $x > -3$, but combined with the upper bound: $-3 < x < 15$. If $x$ must be positive, then $0 < x < 15$.

Equilateral Triangle Area

Formula & Derivation

Concept
$$A = \frac{s^2\sqrt{3}}{4}$$

This comes from splitting the equilateral triangle into two 30-60-90 triangles. The height is $h = \frac{s\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Practice

(Easy) Find the area of an equilateral triangle with side length 6.

$A = \frac{6^2\sqrt{3}}{4} = \frac{36\sqrt{3}}{4} = 9\sqrt{3}$

(ACT-Hard) An equilateral triangle has area $25\sqrt{3}$. What is its side length?

$\frac{s^2\sqrt{3}}{4} = 25\sqrt{3}$

$s^2 = 100 \implies s = 10$

Quadrilateral Areas

Rectangles, Parallelograms & Trapezoids

Concept

$$A_{\text{trap}} = \frac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2) \cdot h$$

Parallelogram trap: The height $h$ is not the slanted side — it's the perpendicular distance between the bases!

Worked Example

A trapezoid has parallel bases of 8 cm and 14 cm, with a height of 5 cm. Find the area.

Given: $b_1 = 8$, $b_2 = 14$, $h = 5$

$A = \frac{1}{2}(8 + 14)(5) = \frac{1}{2}(22)(5) = 55$ cm²

Practice

(Easy) A rectangle has length 12 and width 5. Find its area and perimeter.

$A = 12 \times 5 = 60$. $P = 2(12) + 2(5) = 34$.

(Medium) A parallelogram has base 10 and area 70. What is its height?

$A = bh \implies 70 = 10h \implies h = 7$.

(ACT-Hard) A trapezoid has an area of 84 cm² and a height of 7 cm. One base is 10 cm. Find the other base.

$84 = \frac{1}{2}(10 + b_2)(7)$

$84 = \frac{7}{2}(10 + b_2)$

$24 = 10 + b_2$

$b_2 = 14$ cm

Trapezoid Midsegment

Midsegment Theorem

Concept

The midsegment connects the midpoints of the two non-parallel sides (legs).

$$m = \frac{b_1 + b_2}{2}$$

It is parallel to both bases and its length is the average of the two bases.

Worked Example

A trapezoid has bases of 6 and 18. What is the length of the midsegment?

$m = \frac{6 + 18}{2} = \frac{24}{2} = 12$

Practice

(Easy) A trapezoid has bases of 10 and 22. Find the midsegment.

$m = \frac{10+22}{2} = 16$

(Medium) A trapezoid's midsegment is 15 and one base is 12. Find the other base.

$15 = \frac{12 + b_2}{2}$

$30 = 12 + b_2 \implies b_2 = 18$

(ACT-Hard) A trapezoid has bases of $(3x-1)$ and $(5x+3)$. The midsegment has length $4x + 5$. Find $x$.

$4x+5 = \frac{(3x-1)+(5x+3)}{2} = \frac{8x+2}{2} = 4x+1$

$4x+5 = 4x+1$

$5 = 1$ — No solution! No such trapezoid exists.

Polygon Angles

Interior & Exterior Angles

Concept

For an $n$-sided polygon:

Interior angle sum: $(n - 2) \cdot 180°$

Each interior angle of a regular polygon: $\frac{(n-2) \cdot 180°}{n}$

Exterior angles always sum to $360°$. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon: $\frac{360°}{n}$

Worked Example

What is the measure of each interior angle of a regular octagon?

Given: $n = 8$

Interior angle sum $= (8-2) \times 180° = 1080°$

Each angle $= \frac{1080°}{8} = 135°$

Practice

(Easy) What is the sum of interior angles of a hexagon ($n = 6$)?

$(6-2) \times 180° = 720°$

(Medium) Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 40°. How many sides does it have?

$n = \frac{360°}{40°} = 9$ sides.

(ACT-Hard) The interior angle sum of a polygon is 1440°. What is each interior angle if it's regular?

$(n-2) \times 180 = 1440$

$n - 2 = 8 \implies n = 10$

Each angle $= \frac{1440°}{10} = 144°$

Circle Basics: Circumference & Area

Core Formulas

Concept

$$C = 2\pi r = \pi d \qquad A = \pi r^2$$

Worked Example

A circle has radius 5. Find its circumference and area.

$C = 2\pi(5) = 10\pi \approx 31.4$

$A = \pi(5)^2 = 25\pi \approx 78.5$

Practice

(Easy) A circle has diameter 20. What is its area?

$r = 10$. $A = \pi(10)^2 = 100\pi$.

(Medium) The circumference of a circle is $18\pi$. What is its area?

$2\pi r = 18\pi \implies r = 9$

$A = \pi(9)^2 = 81\pi$

(ACT-Hard) A circle's area is $49\pi$. A square is inscribed in this circle. What is the area of the square?

$\pi r^2 = 49\pi \implies r = 7$, so diameter $= 14$.

The diagonal of the inscribed square = diameter = 14.

For a square: diagonal $= s\sqrt{2}$, so $s = \frac{14}{\sqrt{2}} = 7\sqrt{2}$.

Area $= s^2 = (7\sqrt{2})^2 = 98$.

Arc Length & Sector Area

The "Fraction of the Circle" Approach

Concept

The fraction of the circle is $\frac{\theta}{360°}$. Apply it to circumference or area:

In degrees:

$$\text{Arc length} = \frac{\theta}{360°} \cdot 2\pi r \qquad \text{Sector area} = \frac{\theta}{360°} \cdot \pi r^2$$

In radians ($\theta$ in rad):

$$\text{Arc length} = r\theta \qquad \text{Sector area} = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$$

Worked Example

A circle has radius 12 and a central angle of 90°. Find the arc length and sector area.

Using degrees:

Fraction: $\frac{90}{360} = \frac{1}{4}$

Arc length $= \frac{1}{4} \cdot 2\pi(12) = 6\pi$

Sector area $= \frac{1}{4} \cdot \pi(12)^2 = 36\pi$

Using radians:

Convert: $90° = \frac{\pi}{2}$ rad

Arc length $= r\theta = 12 \cdot \frac{\pi}{2} = 6\pi$ ✓

Sector area $= \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta = \frac{1}{2}(144)\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\right) = 36\pi$ ✓

Practice

(Easy) A pizza has radius 8 inches. A slice has a central angle of 45°. What is the area of the slice?

Degrees: $A = \frac{45}{360} \cdot \pi(8)^2 = \frac{1}{8} \cdot 64\pi = 8\pi \approx 25.1$ in²

Radians: $45° = \frac{\pi}{4}$ rad. $A = \frac{1}{2}(64)\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right) = 8\pi$ ✓

(Medium) An arc has length $5\pi$ and the radius is 10. What is the central angle?

Degrees: $5\pi = \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 2\pi(10) = \frac{\theta}{360} \cdot 20\pi$

$\frac{5\pi}{20\pi} = \frac{\theta}{360} \implies \frac{1}{4} = \frac{\theta}{360} \implies \theta = 90°$

Radians: $s = r\theta \implies 5\pi = 10\theta \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{2}$ rad $= 90°$ ✓

(ACT-Hard) A sector has area $12\pi$ and a central angle of 120°. What is the radius?

Degrees: $12\pi = \frac{120}{360} \cdot \pi r^2 = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 \implies r^2 = 36 \implies r = 6$

Radians: $120° = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ rad. $12\pi = \frac{1}{2}r^2\!\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) = \frac{\pi r^2}{3} \implies r^2 = 36 \implies r = 6$ ✓

Central & Inscribed Angles

Angle-Arc Relationships

Concept

Central angle = intercepted arc.

Inscribed angle = $\frac{1}{2}$ × intercepted arc.

An inscribed angle that intercepts a semicircle (diameter) is always 90°.

Practice

(Easy) A central angle is 80°. What is the intercepted arc?

The arc = 80° (central angle equals its arc).

(Medium) An inscribed angle intercepts an arc of 140°. What is the inscribed angle?

Inscribed angle $= \frac{140°}{2} = 70°$.

(ACT-Hard) An inscribed angle and a central angle both intercept the same arc. The central angle is $4x + 10$ and the inscribed angle is $x + 15$. Find $x$.

The inscribed angle is half the central angle:

$x + 15 = \frac{4x+10}{2} = 2x+5$

$15 - 5 = 2x - x \implies x = 10$

Central angle $= 50°$, inscribed angle $= 25°$. ✓

Equation of a Circle & Completing the Square

Standard Form

Concept

Standard form of a circle:

$$(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2$$

Center: $(h, k)$    Radius: $r$

Sign trap: $(x - 3)^2$ means $h = 3$ (positive), and $(x + 2)^2 = (x - (-2))^2$ means $h = -2$ (negative). Watch the signs!

Interactive Visualizer

Use the sliders below to see how $h$, $k$, and $r$ change the circle in real time:

$h$ — shifts the center left/right. Increasing $h$ moves the circle right.

$k$ — shifts the center up/down. Increasing $k$ moves the circle up.

$r$ — changes the radius (size of the circle).

Try dragging the sliders and watch the circle move and resize on the graph!

Completing the Square — Step by Step

Concept

When a circle equation is given in general form like $x^2 + y^2 + Dx + Ey + F = 0$, you must complete the square to convert it to standard form.

Worked Example

Find the center and radius: $x^2 + y^2 + 6x - 4y - 12 = 0$

Step 1: Group $x$ and $y$ terms, move constant

$(x^2 + 6x) + (y^2 - 4y) = 12$

Step 2: Complete the square for $x$

Take half of 6 → 3. Square it → 9. Add 9 to both sides.

$(x^2 + 6x + 9) + (y^2 - 4y) = 12 + 9$

Step 3: Complete the square for $y$

Take half of −4 → −2. Square it → 4. Add 4 to both sides.

$(x^2 + 6x + 9) + (y^2 - 4y + 4) = 12 + 9 + 4$

Step 4: Factor and simplify

$(x + 3)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 25$

Answer: Center $= (-3, 2)$, Radius $= \sqrt{25} = 5$

The recipe: For each variable — take half the coefficient, square it, add to both sides. Then factor each group into a perfect square binomial.

Practice

(Easy) What are the center and radius of $(x - 4)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 36$?

Center $= (4, -1)$, Radius $= 6$.

(Medium) Find the center and radius: $x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 2y + 10 = 0$

$(x^2 - 10x) + (y^2 + 2y) = -10$

$(x^2 - 10x + 25) + (y^2 + 2y + 1) = -10 + 25 + 1$

$(x - 5)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 16$

Center $= (5, -1)$, Radius $= 4$.

(ACT-Hard) Find the center and radius: $x^2 + y^2 + 8x - 14y + 40 = 0$

$(x^2 + 8x) + (y^2 - 14y) = -40$

$(x^2 + 8x + 16) + (y^2 - 14y + 49) = -40 + 16 + 49$

$(x + 4)^2 + (y - 7)^2 = 25$

Center $= (-4, 7)$, Radius $= 5$.

Distance, Midpoint & Slope

The Big Three Coordinate Formulas

Concept

Given: Two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$

Distance (Pythagorean theorem on the coordinate plane):

The horizontal and vertical distances between two points form the legs of a right triangle. We call these $\Delta x$ and $\Delta y$ ("change in $x$" and "change in $y$"):

$$\Delta x = x_2 - x_1 \qquad \Delta y = y_2 - y_1$$

The distance $d$ is the hypotenuse, so by the Pythagorean theorem:

$$d = \sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2} = \sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}$$

Midpoint (average of coordinates):

$$M = \left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\; \frac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$$

Slope (rise over run):

$$m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$$
Worked Example

Find the distance, midpoint, and slope between $A(1, 3)$ and $B(7, 11)$.

Distance: $d = \sqrt{(7-1)^2+(11-3)^2} = \sqrt{36+64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$

Midpoint: $M = \left(\frac{1+7}{2}, \frac{3+11}{2}\right) = (4, 7)$

Slope: $m = \frac{11-3}{7-1} = \frac{8}{6} = \frac{4}{3}$

Practice

(Easy) Find the midpoint of $(2, 8)$ and $(10, 4)$.

$M = \left(\frac{2+10}{2}, \frac{8+4}{2}\right) = (6, 6)$

(Medium) Find the distance between $(-3, 2)$ and $(1, -1)$.

$d = \sqrt{(1-(-3))^2 + (-1-2)^2} = \sqrt{16+9} = \sqrt{25} = 5$

(ACT-Hard) The midpoint of segment $\overline{AB}$ is $(5, -2)$. If $A = (3, 4)$, find $B$.

$\frac{3 + x_B}{2} = 5 \implies x_B = 7$

$\frac{4 + y_B}{2} = -2 \implies y_B = -8$

$B = (7, -8)$

Equations of Lines

Slope-Intercept, Point-Slope & Parallel/Perpendicular

Concept

Slope-Intercept: $y = mx + b$   ($m$ = slope, $b$ = y-intercept)

Point-Slope: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$   (when you know a point and slope)

Parallel lines: same slope → $m_1 = m_2$

Perpendicular lines: negative reciprocal slopes → $m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1$

Worked Example

Write the equation of a line perpendicular to $y = 2x + 3$ passing through $(4, 1)$.

Given slope: $m = 2$

Perpendicular slope: $m_\perp = -\frac{1}{2}$

Point-slope: $y - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}(x - 4)$

$y - 1 = -\frac{1}{2}x + 2$

$y = -\frac{1}{2}x + 3$

Practice

(Easy) What is the slope of a line parallel to $y = -3x + 7$?

Parallel → same slope: $m = -3$.

(Medium) Write the equation of a line through $(2, 5)$ with slope $\frac{3}{4}$.

$y - 5 = \frac{3}{4}(x - 2)$

$y = \frac{3}{4}x - \frac{3}{2} + 5 = \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{7}{2}$

(ACT-Hard) Line $\ell$ passes through $(1, 4)$ and $(3, -2)$. Write the equation of a line perpendicular to $\ell$ through the midpoint of those two points.

Slope of $\ell$: $m = \frac{-2-4}{3-1} = \frac{-6}{2} = -3$

Perpendicular slope: $m_\perp = \frac{1}{3}$

Midpoint: $\left(\frac{1+3}{2}, \frac{4+(-2)}{2}\right) = (2, 1)$

Equation: $y - 1 = \frac{1}{3}(x - 2) \implies y = \frac{1}{3}x + \frac{1}{3}$

SOH-CAH-TOA

Right Triangle Trig Ratios

Concept

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\color{#E57373}{\text{opposite}}}{\color{#21442B}{\text{hypotenuse}}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\color{#4A90D9}{\text{adjacent}}}{\color{#21442B}{\text{hypotenuse}}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\color{#E57373}{\text{opposite}}}{\color{#4A90D9}{\text{adjacent}}}$$

Memory trick: "Some Old Hippie Caught Another Hippie Tripping On Acid" — the first letters spell out SOH-CAH-TOA.

Worked Example

In a right triangle, the side opposite angle $A$ is 5 and the hypotenuse is 13. Find $\sin A$, $\cos A$, and $\tan A$.

Given: opp = 5, hyp = 13

Find adj: $5^2 + \text{adj}^2 = 13^2 \implies \text{adj} = 12$ (5-12-13 triple)

$\sin A = \frac{5}{13} \qquad \cos A = \frac{12}{13} \qquad \tan A = \frac{5}{12}$

Practice

(Easy) In a right triangle with legs 3 and 4 and hypotenuse 5, find $\sin\theta$ where $\theta$ is the angle opposite the side of length 3.

$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}} = \frac{3}{5}$

(Medium) $\tan\theta = \frac{7}{24}$. Find $\sin\theta$.

opp = 7, adj = 24. hyp $= \sqrt{7^2+24^2} = \sqrt{49+576} = \sqrt{625} = 25$.

$\sin\theta = \frac{7}{25}$

(ACT-Hard) From a point on the ground 40 feet from a building, the angle of elevation to the top of the building is 55°. How tall is the building? (Use $\tan 55° \approx 1.43$)

$\tan 55° = \frac{\text{height}}{40}$

height $= 40 \times \tan 55° = 40 \times 1.43 = 57.2$ feet

On the unit circle, for any angle $\theta$: the $x$-coordinate $= \cos\theta$ and the $y$-coordinate $= \sin\theta$.

The dashed triangle above shows why: drop a vertical line from the point to the $x$-axis and you get a right triangle. The horizontal leg is $\cos\theta$, the vertical leg is $\sin\theta$, and the hypotenuse (the radius) is 1.

Key Values to Memorize

$\theta$ $0°$ $30°$ $45°$ $60°$ $90°$
$\sin\theta$ $0$ $\frac{1}{2}$ $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ $1$
$\cos\theta$ $1$ $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ $\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ $\frac{1}{2}$ $0$
$\tan\theta$ $0$ $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ $1$ $\sqrt{3}$ undef.
Practice

(Easy) What is $\cos 60°$?

$\cos 60° = \frac{1}{2}$

(Medium) Convert $150°$ to radians.

$150° \times \frac{\pi}{180°} = \frac{5\pi}{6}$

(ACT-Hard) If $\sin\theta = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\theta$ is in Quadrant II, find $\cos\theta$.

$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$

$\frac{9}{25} + \cos^2\theta = 1 \implies \cos^2\theta = \frac{16}{25}$

$\cos\theta = \pm\frac{4}{5}$. In Quadrant II, cosine is negative.

$\cos\theta = -\frac{4}{5}$

Trig Graphs & Transformations

Amplitude, Period & Shifts

Concept

For $y = a\sin(bx - c) + d$ or $y = a\cos(bx - c) + d$:

Amplitude: $|a|$ (vertical stretch — how tall the wave is)

Period: $\frac{2\pi}{|b|}$ (horizontal stretch — how wide one cycle is)

Phase shift: $\frac{c}{b}$ (horizontal slide)

Vertical shift: $d$ (moves the midline up or down)

Interactive Visualizer

Drag the sliders to see how each parameter transforms the sine and cosine curves:

$a$ — controls amplitude. Larger $|a|$ = taller wave. Negative $a$ flips the curve.

$b$ — controls period. Larger $|b|$ = more cycles squeezed in (shorter period $= \frac{2\pi}{|b|}$).

$c$ — controls phase shift. Slides the wave left or right by $\frac{c}{b}$.

$d$ — controls vertical shift. Moves the entire wave up or down.

Worked Example

For $y = 3\sin(2x) + 1$, find the amplitude, period, and vertical shift.

$a = 3$, $b = 2$, $c = 0$, $d = 1$

Amplitude $= |3| = 3$

Period $= \frac{2\pi}{2} = \pi$

Vertical shift: up 1 (midline at $y = 1$)

Practice

(Easy) What is the period of $y = \sin(4x)$?

Period $= \frac{2\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2}$

(Medium) What is the amplitude and period of $y = -5\cos\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)$?

Amplitude $= |-5| = 5$

Period $= \frac{2\pi}{1/3} = 6\pi$

(ACT-Hard) A trig function has amplitude 2, period $4\pi$, and a vertical shift of $-3$. Write a possible equation.

$a = 2$, $d = -3$

Period $= \frac{2\pi}{b} = 4\pi \implies b = \frac{1}{2}$

$y = 2\sin\left(\frac{x}{2}\right) - 3$ (or cosine works too)

Mixed Review — ACT-Level Problems

Directions: These problems are mixed across all geometry topics, just like the real ACT. Try each one before checking the solution. Problems are shuffled each time you reload the page!

1

A circle has equation $x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y - 11 = 0$. What is its radius?

CIRCLES — COMPLETING THE SQUARE

$(x^2-6x)+(y^2+8y)=11$

$(x^2-6x+9)+(y^2+8y+16)=11+9+16$

$(x-3)^2+(y+4)^2=36$

Radius $= \sqrt{36} = 6$

2

In a right triangle, $\sin A = \frac{8}{17}$. What is $\cos A$?

TRIG — SOH-CAH-TOA + PYTHAGOREAN

opp = 8, hyp = 17. adj $= \sqrt{17^2-8^2} = \sqrt{289-64} = \sqrt{225} = 15$

$\cos A = \frac{15}{17}$

3

The interior angle sum of a polygon is $2340°$. How many sides does the polygon have?

POLYGONS — ANGLE SUM

$(n-2) \times 180 = 2340$

$n-2 = 13 \implies n = 15$ sides

4

A trapezoid has bases 9 and 21 and a midsegment of length $m$. What is the area of the trapezoid if the height is 8?

TRAPEZOID — MIDSEGMENT + AREA

$m = \frac{9+21}{2} = 15$

Fun shortcut: Area $= m \times h = 15 \times 8 = 120$

Or: $A = \frac{1}{2}(9+21)(8) = \frac{1}{2}(30)(8) = 120$ ✓

5

Line $\ell$ has equation $y = \frac{2}{3}x - 4$. What is the slope of a line perpendicular to $\ell$?

COORDINATE — PERPENDICULAR SLOPES

Slope of $\ell$: $m = \frac{2}{3}$

Perpendicular slope: $m_\perp = -\frac{3}{2}$

(Flip the fraction and change the sign.)

6

An equilateral triangle has side length 8. Find its height and area.

TRIANGLES — EQUILATERAL

Height: $h = \frac{s\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{8\sqrt{3}}{2} = 4\sqrt{3}$

Area: $A = \frac{s^2\sqrt{3}}{4} = \frac{64\sqrt{3}}{4} = 16\sqrt{3}$

7

A sector of a circle has a central angle of $150°$ and radius $6$. What is the area of the sector?

CIRCLES — SECTOR AREA

$A = \frac{150}{360} \cdot \pi(6)^2 = \frac{5}{12} \cdot 36\pi = 15\pi$

8

The hypotenuse of a 45-45-90 triangle is $10\sqrt{2}$. What is the area of the triangle?

SPECIAL RIGHT TRIANGLES

hyp $= s\sqrt{2} = 10\sqrt{2} \implies s = 10$

Both legs = 10.

$A = \frac{1}{2}(10)(10) = 50$

9

What is the distance between $(−2, 5)$ and $(4, −3)$?

COORDINATE — DISTANCE FORMULA

$d = \sqrt{(4-(-2))^2+(-3-5)^2} = \sqrt{36+64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$

10

A cylinder has radius 4 and height 9. What is its volume?

3D SOLIDS — CYLINDER

$V = \pi r^2 h = \pi(4)^2(9) = \pi(16)(9) = 144\pi$
