Core moves

Desmos is available throughout the SAT Math section in Bluebook. The goal is to recognize when a graph can help, enter the equation accurately, and understand what the result tells you. This guide introduces nine useful skills with examples and the exact entries to practice. The examples were checked in the College Board version of Desmos. As you practice, start by identifying the question type. Then decide whether Desmos or algebra makes more sense, and check that your answer addresses what the question asks.

★ Essential: the Desmos skills you will use most often. Start here.Unstarred: comes up less often. Come back to these after you feel comfortable with the starred skills.
★Move 1Type it the way the test prints it

Desmos can graph equations in many forms, so you often do not need to rearrange first. Standard form, a circle, an equation with $x$ on both sides: type it as printed. A few entry habits help Desmos read what you meant.

★Move 2Read the gray dots

Click a curve and Desmos marks points that SAT questions often ask about: where the curve crosses each axis, and where it reaches a high or low point. Click a dot to see its coordinates. Zeros, roots, $x$-intercepts and the solutions of $f(x) = 0$ are four names for the same dots.

Type this in Desmos
1
$y = x^2 - 2x - 8$
click the curve: dots at (−2, 0) and (4, 0) on the x-axis, (0, −8) on the y-axis, and the vertex (1, −9)
zeros, roots
the dots on the $x$-axis
$y$-intercept
the dot on the $y$-axis
max or min
the dot at the vertex: its $x$ says where, its $y$ is the value
a circle
dots at the top and the bottom: the center is halfway between them, and the radius is half the distance
Example A$y = 2x^2 - 9$: the right-hand dot reads $(2.121, 0)$. Algebra agrees: $x = \sqrt{4.5} \approx 2.121$.
Example B$h(x) = -2(x - 1)(x - 7)$: the vertex dot reads $(4, 18)$. The maximum value is $18$, and it happens at $x = 4$. Reread the question to see which of the two it wants.
Example C$(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$: the top dot is $(3, 3)$ and the bottom dot is $(3, -7)$. Halfway between is the center, $(3, -2)$, and half the distance is the radius, $5$.
Desmos rounds what it shows. When a dot reads $2.121$, the exact answer may be $\sqrt{4.5}$ or $\tfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$. Match it to the choices by typing each choice and comparing decimals.
★Move 3Intersections solve systems, and many single equations

The solution of a system is the point where its graphs cross. The same idea works for a single equation: graph the left side and the right side as two curves, and the $x$-coordinates of the crossings are the solutions.

Type this in Desmos
1
$3x + 2y = 16$
2
$y = x - 2$
click the crossing: (4, 2)
a system
one equation per line, then click the crossing
a single equation
$y =$ left side on one line, $y =$ right side on the next; read the $x$ of each crossing
one variable
or type the equation itself, such as $3(x - 2) + 5 = 2x + 7$: a vertical line appears at each solution
finish the question
it may want $x$, $y$, $x + y$, or the sum of the solutions
Example A$|2x - 3| = 11$: graph $y = |2x - 3|$ and $y = 11$. They cross at $x = -4$ and $x = 7$, so the sum of the solutions is $3$.
Example B$3(x - 2) + 5 = 2x + 7$, typed as printed, draws a vertical line at $x = 8$.
Example C$\sqrt{x + 9} = x - 3$: the two graphs cross once, at $x = 7$. Squaring both sides by hand also produces $x = 0$, which does not satisfy the original equation. The graphs do not cross there, so the picture shows only $x = 7$.
This is one of the most useful Desmos skills for the SAT. In many cases, two equations and an intersection give you the information you need.
★Move 4Count the solutions

Some questions ask how many solutions there are rather than what the solutions are. A graph can help you see the number of intersections, as long as you interpret it carefully.

What you seeSolutionsWhy
two lines that crossexactly onedifferent slopes
two parallel linesnonesame slope, different intercepts
one line, though you typed twoinfinitely manyboth equations describe the same line
a line and a parabolanone, one, or twocount the crossings; a line that just touches counts once
Example A$4x - 6y = 8$ and $2x - 3y = 4$: only one line appears, because the first equation is the second one doubled. Infinitely many solutions.
Example B$x - 2y = 3$ and $3x - 6y = 4$: two parallel lines. No solution.
Example C$y = x^2 - 4x + 7$ and $y = 2x - 2$: the line touches the parabola at $(3, 4)$ and nowhere else. Exactly one solution.
Zoom out before you decide that two lines never meet. Lines with close slopes can look parallel in the default window and still cross off screen.

Common mistakes and what to check

These are common entry and interpretation mistakes. Review them so you know what to check when an answer seems off. Practice spotting these now so they are easier to catch on test day.

Common mistake 1leaving the test’s letters in
Type $3a - 2b = 12$ and Desmos offers sliders for $a$ and $b$ where you wanted a graph. Retype the system with $x$ and $y$.
Common mistake 2forgetting the right arrow after a fraction
y=3/4x−2 graphs $y = \dfrac{3}{4x - 2}$. Press the right arrow to leave the denominator, and read your line back before you rely on the graph.
Common mistake 3answering $x$ when the question asks for something else
The crossing is $(4, 2)$ and the question wants $y$, or $x + y$, or the sum of two solutions. Finding the point is only one step. Check what the question asks you to do with it.
Common mistake 4calling it “no solution” from a zoomed-in window
Two lines with close slopes can look parallel near the origin. Zoom out, or compare the slopes, before you choose “zero.”
Common mistake 5picking a point on a dashed edge
With $<$ or $>$, the boundary line is dashed and its points are not solutions. If $b < 6$, the greatest integer is $5$, not $6$.
Common mistake 6rounding a decimal the answer box will not take
When Desmos shows a rounded decimal such as $0.6667$, enter $2/3$ or $.6667$ rather than $.67$. The Bluebook Tools tab on this sheet has practice with the entry rules.
Common mistake 7graphing what a glance would answer
The $y$-intercept of $y = 3x^2 - 12x + 7$ is $7$: read the constant directly. Use Desmos when it helps; for simpler questions, algebra may be quicker.

Student-Produced Responses (Grid-Ins)

These are called Student Produced Responses (AKA grid-ins). You type in the answer yourself. Your entry needs to follow Bluebook’s answer-format rules, so it is worth practicing the format before test day.

Fraction or decimal

Either one counts.
1/2, .5 and 0.5 are all correct.

Long decimals

If it does not fit, fill every space. For two thirds, .6666, .6667, 0.666 or 0.667 count. .67 does not.

Mixed numbers

A whole number plus a fraction, like 3 and one half (written 3½), is read as 31/2. Type 7/2 or 3.5.

No symbols

No percent % signs, dollar $ signs or commas. If the answer is 25 percent, type 25.

Negatives

The same rules apply. Type the minus sign first.

More than one answer

Enter one of them, not a list like “0.5 or ½”.

Use the Answer Preview. Bluebook shows a preview of how it read your entry. Check it each time; it can help you catch a stray character or a mistyped fraction before you move on. Answers can be at most 5 characters if positive, 6 if negative (the minus sign counts).

Do They Provide Any Formulas?

Yes. Bluebook has a reference sheet you can open and close during the Math section. It covers geometry and basic trig. It does not cover algebra, so it helps to know the quadratic formula, slope, exponent rules, and similar formulas before test day. Here is what the reference sheet includes:

Circle

$$A = \pi r^{2} \qquad C = 2\pi r$$

Rectangle & Triangle

$$A = \ell w \qquad A = \tfrac{1}{2}bh$$

Right Triangle

$$c^{2} = a^{2} + b^{2}$$

Pythagorean theorem

Special Right Triangles

$$30\text{–}60\text{–}90:\ x,\ x\sqrt{3},\ 2x$$ $$45\text{–}45\text{–}90:\ s,\ s,\ s\sqrt{2}$$

Box & Cylinder

$$V = \ell w h \qquad V = \pi r^{2} h$$

Sphere

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^{3}$$

Cone & Pyramid

$$V = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi r^{2} h \qquad V = \tfrac{1}{3}\ell w h$$

Angle Facts

$$360^{\circ} = 2\pi \text{ radians in a circle}$$ $$\text{Triangle angles sum to } 180^{\circ}$$

Review these formulas separately. They are not included on the reference sheet: quadratic formula $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^{2} - 4ac}}{2a}$, vertex $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$, slope $m = \dfrac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1}$, distance $\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2 + (y_2-y_1)^2}$, midpoint $\left(\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \dfrac{y_1+y_2}{2}\right)$, exponential growth $A = P(1 + r)^{t}$, circle $(x-h)^2 + (y-k)^2 = r^2$.
