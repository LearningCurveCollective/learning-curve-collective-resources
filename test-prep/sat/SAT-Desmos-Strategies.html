Can I Use a Calculator?

Yes — and on the digital SAT you can use one on every Math question. There is no longer a no-calculator section. You may bring your own approved scientific or graphing calculator (the College Board keeps the current approved list on its calculator policy page), but a Desmos graphing calculator is also built directly into the Bluebook testing app.

Because the built-in calculator is always one click away, the smartest habit is to know exactly which kinds of problems Desmos handles faster than algebra. That is what this sheet trains. Bring the calculator you are comfortable with, but learn the Desmos moves below — several of them are things a handheld simply can't do.

Two ways to solve is a safety net. The SAT gives you a fairly generous amount of time per Math question. On a question you're unsure about, solve it algebraically on scratch paper and confirm it on the graph. If the two answers agree, move on with confidence.

Graphing to Find Points

The first seven techniques all rely on one fact: Desmos automatically marks the points that matter. Intercepts, vertices, and intersections show up as grey dots. Hover (or tap) a dot and its coordinates appear. Most of the work is simply typing the equation correctly.

1 Visualizing Important Points on a Function

Concept

Any intercept or vertex you'd normally solve for algebraically can be read directly off the graph. Desmos highlights them for you.

Example Question
Fill-in
In the parabola $y = 3x^{2} - 5$, what is the $x$-coordinate of the positive $x$-intercept? Round to the nearest thousandth.

Given:

  • $y = 3x^{2} - 5$
  • Want the positive $x$-intercept, i.e. where $y = 0$ and $x > 0$

Type the equation. Two grey dots appear where the parabola crosses the $x$-axis. Hover the one on the right: it reads $(1.291,\,0)$.

Algebra check: $3x^{2} = 5 \;\Rightarrow\; x = \sqrt{5/3} \approx 1.291$.

Answer: 1.291

2 Finding the Solution to a System of Equations

Concept

The solution to a system of two equations is the point where their graphs meet. Enter each equation on its own line and read the intersection.

Example Question
Fill-in
$$2x + 5y = 9$$ $$y = 2x - 3$$ The solution to the system above is $(x,\,y)$. What is the value of $x$?

Given:

  • Line 1: $2x + 5y = 9$
  • Line 2: $y = 2x - 3$
  • Want the $x$-value of the intersection

Both lines go in exactly as written — Desmos handles standard form and slope-intercept form equally well. The lines cross at $(2,\,1)$, so $x = 2$.

Algebra check: substitute $y = 2x - 3$: $2x + 5(2x-3) = 9 \Rightarrow 12x = 24 \Rightarrow x = 2$.

Answer: 2
Watch the letters. If the test writes the system in $a$ and $b$, retype it with $x$ and $y$. The intersection is the same; Desmos just needs those two names to draw a graph.

3 Finding Points of Intersection with an Axis

Concept

"Where does $f(x)$ cross the $x$-axis?" is the same question as "what are the zeros of $f$?" — and the graph shows every zero at once, including ones that are hard to factor.

Example Question
Fill-in
At what values of $x$ does the function $f(x) = 3x^{2} - 27$ intersect the $x$-axis?

Given:

  • $f(x) = 3x^{2} - 27$
  • Want all $x$ with $f(x) = 0$

Graph it and hover the two grey dots on the $x$-axis: $(-3,\,0)$ and $(3,\,0)$.

Algebra check: $3x^{2} = 27 \Rightarrow x^{2} = 9 \Rightarrow x = \pm 3$.

Answer: x = 3 and x = −3

4 Determining the Number of Solutions to a System

Concept

Two lines can cross once (one solution), run parallel (no solutions), or lie on top of each other (infinitely many). When neither equation is in slope-intercept form, graphing is much faster than converting.

Example Question
Multiple choice
How many solutions does the following system have? $$3x + 2y = 7$$ $$5x - 4y = 8$$
  • AZero
  • BExactly one
  • CExactly two
  • DInfinitely many

Given:

  • $3x + 2y = 7$ and $5x - 4y = 8$
  • Want the number of $(x,y)$ pairs satisfying both

Type each equation on its own line. The two lines have clearly different slopes and cross exactly once, at $(2,\,0.5)$. One intersection means one solution.

Answer: B — exactly one
Zoom out before deciding "no solutions." Two lines with nearly equal slopes can look parallel in the default window but cross far off-screen. Compare the slopes ($-\tfrac{3}{2}$ vs. $\tfrac{5}{4}$ here) if the picture is ambiguous.

5 Graphing a Circle

Concept

The standard form $(x-h)^{2} + (y-k)^{2} = r^{2}$ goes into Desmos exactly as written. Questions about where a circle sits relative to the axes become a quick look.

Example Question
Fill-in
How many times does the graph of the circle with the equation below intersect the $x$-axis? $$(x - 1)^{2} + (y - 5)^{2} = 16$$

Given:

  • Center $(h,k) = (1,\,5)$, radius $r = \sqrt{16} = 4$
  • Want the number of $x$-axis crossings

Graph it. The lowest point of the circle is at $y = 5 - 4 = 1$, which is still above the $x$-axis, so the circle never touches it.

Answer: 0

6 Minimum and Maximum

Concept

The maximum or minimum of a parabola is its vertex, and Desmos flags the vertex automatically. Hover it and read the coordinates — no completing the square needed.

Example Question
Fill-in
$$f(x) = -(x + 4)(x - 2)$$ The function $f$ is defined above. For which value of $x$ does $f(x)$ reach its maximum?

Given:

  • $f(x) = -(x+4)(x-2)$, a downward-opening parabola
  • Want the $x$-value of the vertex

Graph the function and drag the view until the highest point is visible. The vertex dot reads $(-1,\,9)$. The question asks for the $x$-value, so the answer is $-1$ — not $9$.

Algebra check: the zeros are $x = -4$ and $x = 2$; the vertex sits at their midpoint, $\dfrac{-4 + 2}{2} = -1$.

Answer: −1

7 Translation

Concept

Shifting a graph up or down moves every point by the same amount. Graph the original, note where the vertex is, and reason about where the shifted vertex would land — or just graph the shifted version too.

Example Question
Fill-in
$$f(x) = 3x^{2} - 12x + 7$$ The function above is translated up 2 units in the $xy$-plane. How many times does the translated function intersect the $x$-axis?

Given:

  • $f(x) = 3x^{2} - 12x + 7$
  • Translated graph: $g(x) = f(x) + 2$
  • Want the number of $x$-intercepts of $g$

Graph $f$ and hover the vertex: $(2,\,-5)$. Moving up 2 puts the new vertex at $(2,\,-3)$ — still below the axis, and the parabola opens upward, so it must cross the axis twice. Typing $y = f(x) + 2$ as a second line confirms it visually.

Answer: 2
Shortcut: once you've defined $f(x)$ on one line, Desmos lets you reuse it. $y = f(x) + 2$, $y = f(x - 3)$, $y = -f(x)$ all graph instantly.

Beyond Plain Graphing

These six techniques use features a handheld calculator either lacks or buries in menus: list statistics, shaded inequalities, sliders for unknown constants, and one-click regression.

8 Mean and Median

Concept

Desmos has list functions built in. Type mean( or median( followed by the numbers and it evaluates immediately — no sorting, no counting, no arithmetic slips.

Example Question
Fill-in
What are the mean and median of the data set below? $$\{3,\ 7,\ 8,\ 12,\ 15,\ 21\}$$

Given: the six values $3, 7, 8, 12, 15, 21$.

Type mean(3,7,8,12,15,21) → Desmos shows $11$.
Type median(3,7,8,12,15,21) → Desmos shows $10$.

Check: $\text{mean} = \dfrac{66}{6} = 11$; with six values the median is the average of the 3rd and 4th, $\dfrac{8 + 12}{2} = 10$.

Mean 11 · Median 10
Also available: stdev(, min(, max(, total(, and count(. Great for the data-analysis questions.

9 Absolute Value Equations

Concept

An absolute value equation usually has two solutions. Graph the left side and the right side as separate curves; the solutions are the $x$-coordinates of the intersections.

Example Question
Fill-in
What is the sum of the solutions to the equation below? $$|x - 5| = 12$$

Given: $|x - 5| = 12$; want the sum of all solutions.

Graph $y = |x - 5|$ (a V shape) and $y = 12$ (a horizontal line). They cross at $x = -7$ and $x = 17$.

$$(-7) + 17 = 10$$

Algebra check: $x - 5 = 12$ or $x - 5 = -12$, giving $17$ and $-7$.

Answer: 10

10 Inequalities

Concept

Desmos shades the solution region of an inequality. With two inequalities, the answer is wherever the shading overlaps. This turns "find a point that satisfies both" into a look-and-pick task.

Example Question
Fill-in
$$y < x - 2$$ $$y > -x + 5$$ The point $(x,\,4)$ is a solution to the system of inequalities above. What is one possible value of $x$?

Given:

  • $y < x - 2$ and $y > -x + 5$
  • The point has $y = 4$; want any valid $x$

Graph both inequalities, then add the line $y = 4$ as a guide. Along $y = 4$, the doubly-shaded region begins just to the right of $x = 6$. So any $x > 6$ works — for example $x = 8$.

Algebra check: $4 < x - 2 \Rightarrow x > 6$, and $4 > -x + 5 \Rightarrow x > 1$. Both hold when $x > 6$.

Answer: any x > 6 (e.g. 7, 8, 10)
Strict vs. non-strict. With $<$ the boundary line is dashed and not included: $x = 6$ itself is not a solution here. Look for the dashed line before grabbing a boundary value.

11 Checking Solutions

Concept

When the choices are four equations, graph all four. The one whose graph has the properties the question describes is the answer. This is often faster than manipulating each equation by hand.

Example Question
Multiple choice
Which of the following is an equation of a line that has a $y$-intercept of $8$ and an $x$-intercept of $2$?
  • A$2x + 8y = 16$
  • B$4x + y = 8$
  • C$x + 4y = 8$
  • D$8x - 2y = 16$

Given: the line must pass through $(0,\,8)$ and $(2,\,0)$.

Enter each answer choice on its own line. Hover the intercepts of each. Only choice B passes through both $(0,\,8)$ and $(2,\,0)$. Choice A is the trap — it has the intercepts swapped, $(0,\,2)$ and $(8,\,0)$.

Check B: $x=0 \Rightarrow y = 8$ ✓   $y = 0 \Rightarrow 4x = 8 \Rightarrow x = 2$ ✓

Answer: B

12 Evaluating Constants

Concept

If an equation contains an unknown constant like $k$, Desmos offers to create a slider. Drag the slider and watch the graph change until it does what the question describes.

Example Question
Fill-in
$$3x - 6y = 9$$ $$4x + ky = 5$$ In the system of equations above, $k$ is a constant. For what value of $k$ does the system have no solutions?

Given:

  • $3x - 6y = 9$ (fixed line)
  • $4x + ky = 5$ (line whose slope depends on $k$)
  • No solutions $\Leftrightarrow$ the lines are parallel and distinct

Type both equations. After the second one, click add slider: k. Drag $k$ (widen the slider's range if needed) until the two lines are parallel. That happens at $k = -8$.

Algebra check: slope of line 1 is $\dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$; slope of line 2 is $-\dfrac{4}{k}$. Setting $-\dfrac{4}{k} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ gives $k = -8$. The intercepts differ, so the lines don't coincide.

Answer: k = −8
Slider tip: click the endpoints of the slider bar to change its range and step. Setting the step to 1 makes it easy to land on an integer exactly.

13 Slope and $y$-Intercept of a Line from Its Points

Concept

Given a set of points, Desmos can compute the line of best fit in two steps: put the points in a table, then type a regression. The output tells you the slope and intercept directly.

Example Question
Multiple choice
Which of the following best approximates the equation of the line of best fit for the points $(2, 5)$, $(5, 9)$, and $(9, 14)$?
  • A$y = -1.3x + 2.5$
  • B$y = 1.3x + 2.5$
  • C$y = 2.5x + 1.3$
  • D$y = 0.8x + 3.4$

Given: data points $(2,5)$, $(5,9)$, $(9,14)$; want $y = mx + b$ approximately.

Step 1. Click the + button at the top of the expression list and choose table. Enter the $x$-values in the $x_1$ column and the $y$-values in the $y_1$ column.

Step 2. On a new line type y1 ~ m x1 + b. Desmos runs a linear regression and reports:

$$m \approx 1.284, \qquad b \approx 2.486$$

Slope about $1.3$, intercept about $2.5$ — that is choice B. (Notice choice C has the two numbers swapped.)

Answer: B
Not just lines. The same trick fits other models: y1 ~ a x1^2 + b x1 + c for a quadratic, y1 ~ a b^x1 for an exponential. Useful whenever the SAT asks "which function best models the data?"

How Do the Fill-In Questions Work?

Roughly a quarter of the Math questions are student-produced responses — you type an answer instead of picking one. On the digital SAT these are mixed in throughout each module rather than grouped at the end. The rules below prevent avoidable lost points.

More than one right answer is possible
Some questions accept a whole range. If any number from 3 to 7 works, entering $4.5$ (or $3$, or $6$) is fully correct. Enter one answer — never a list or a range.
Repeating decimals: fill every space
Answers can be at most 5 characters if positive, 6 if negative (the minus sign counts). For $\tfrac{5}{9}$ you may enter 5/9, .5555, or .5556. Rounded or truncated short forms like .56 or 0.55 are marked wrong.
You don't have to reduce fractions
$\tfrac{3}{4}$, $\tfrac{6}{8}$, and $\tfrac{9}{12}$ are all scored the same. Don't waste time simplifying — just make sure it fits in the character limit. Mixed numbers are not allowed: enter $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ as 5/2 or 2.5.
Use the Answer Preview
Bluebook shows a preview of how it read your entry. Glance at it every time — it catches a stray character or a fraction typed as a decimal before you submit.

Bottom line: the SAT is not trying to trick you on formatting. A reasonable form of the correct answer, entered carefully, earns the point. Don't enter units, symbols like $\%$ or $\$$, or spaces.
